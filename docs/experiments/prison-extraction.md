# Experiment: prison base + extraction raids (branch experiment/prison-extraction, from main 6cfb26b)

## What exists
- **Base**: a hand-authored 96 x 72 prison compound (`scripts/prison/prison_base.gd`): perimeter fence, two 24 x 15 cell blocks with 24 cells, canteen with kitchen, infirmary, workshop, stores, a walled garden, six watchtowers, a gate with sandbags and a truck. It is built as a TownGen-shaped town dictionary (grids, objects, kit codes), so the real `TownWorld`, `TownRun`/`TownSim` (Ruth, shooting, dormant dead) and renderer run it unchanged.
- **Ten people** (`prison_crew.gd`): Walt and Dez (tower guards), Mia (cook), Marcus and Nia (gardeners), Ana (medic), Hector (mechanic), Tomas (fence crew), Owen (hauler), June (patrol). Real `Sim.Person` objects drawn by the real PawnNode with the real work poses (sort, bench, garden, nurse, mend, haul, radio, watch). They walk routes with the town path finder, work at stations, and each finished stop changes the stores (garden +food, cook -food/-water, hauler +water, mechanic scrap -> ammo, medic scrap -> meds, fence crew -scrap). Floating "+1.5 food" text and a name/job/progress tag over each.
- **Dead at the fence**: 72 dormant records in the pen south of the gate, woken (real zombies, standing/shuffling, `sim.quiet` so they ignore Ruth) by a large wake radius; drawn by the real zombie art.
- **Go out**: E at the gate or truck (cutscene: fade, truck sliding across, caption), then a raid in a generated 64 x 64 town (`TownGen.make(seed, {blocks:2})`): houses, cars, containers, dormant dead that wake near you (the dead within 16 tiles of the drop are removed). The pack is a separate stock: 40 rounds and 1 med taken from the base stores.
- **Raid play**: real avatar, shotgun, melee (F), reload (R), E searches containers/cars/doors (real `sim.uses` and `sim.search`); loot goes to the pack stock. Two pickup rings (the drop-off, armed only after you walk 14+ tiles away, and the far road end), green edge arrows, a 2 s hold.
- **Extract**: cutscene, then the pack (loot + unspent ammo/meds) is added to the base stores and the base is rebuilt; a "Banked: +x" line shows. **Die**: cutscene, pack lost (ammo/med loadout gone), back at base.
- **Menu**: windowed "New game" (town meta, no save, no `--town`) boots the prison. `--onehouse` forces the old game; `--prison` forces this one. Headless runs (tests) are never redirected.

## Fake or missing
- No inventory grid UI: the pack is a plain stock dictionary (food, water, ammo, meds, scrap). The backpack code (`sim.inv`) was not wired.
- NPCs are not in the sim: zombies never target them, they cannot be hurt, no needs, no relations. Bodies are the 5 baked sprites with palette tweaks, so some NPCs share a face. Tower guards stand on a drawn deck; nobody shoots.
- The base has no day/night, no attacks on the fence, no building, no saving. Stores consumption is only the cook's meal cycle. The fence is only a collision and a picture; the dead never get through.
- The compound is a box layout (fence drawn as a thin chain link, no yard variety, no roofs, no lights, flat daylight). The map is not the real prison silhouette.
- Raid map is the small generated town, one per raid, no loot rarity, no weapons variety, no extra ranged enemies. Exits are only road ends. The truck cutscene is drawn rectangles.
- Hunger/thirst of Ruth and the dark look of the One House game are not applied. Raid clock is fixed daytime.

## Verdict
Feasible and surprisingly cheap: the whole loop (base, crew, go out, search, fight, extract, bank, die) took one session because the town stack already does the hard parts. The base is fun to look at for about a minute (people at work, the mass at the fence reads well); the loop's tension is carried by the raid, which already plays like a small extraction shooter (the dead wake as you search). What decides if it is a game is not here yet: why you go out (needs: stores that drain, a goal), what you carry (inventory, risk), and what the base gives back (upgrades). Without those it is a tech demo, but a convincing one.

## Clear phase (branch experiment/prison-clear)
The game now starts in a CLEAR-THE-PRISON phase and then becomes the base-management game. Ruth and three allies (Cole, Bea, Ike) arrive on foot on the west road, outside the fence, and walk in through a breach in the west fence (the gate stays chained, held by the pen). Ten zones (infirmary, both cell blocks, canteen, workshop, stores, garden, north walk, yard, gate area) are defined as rects in `prison_base.gd` (`ZONES`), each with dormant dead (99 in all, they wake by proximity like in the raid town, a little later when you are outside the zone's building), a few containers on real furniture (searched with E, the loot goes straight into the base stores) and one or two crew members. The first zone, the infirmary just inside the west door, has only four dead. A zone is cleared when no awake or dormant dead stand in its rect; then its crew appear there, walk out and start their job (guards walk to their tower and climb it). The HUD shows "Cleared n/10" and the minimap marks zones grey (infested) and green (cleared). When all ten are cleared a "PRISON SECURED" card plays, the base is rebuilt in the managed layout (gate usable, the breach `Base.GAP` is a normal fence tile, all 14 people at work, the dog, day/night, the fence guns and the pen) and `phase` becomes "managed".

Allies follow Ruth and shoot the nearest dead (the tower guards' flash, tracer and rifle sound, `_shoot_at`). They have 45 hp. Dead that reach an ally bite it (8 damage per 0.9 s per dead, up to three), and dead that are nearer to an ally than to Ruth go for the ally (their step toward Ruth is undone and replaced, `_ally_prey_step`). Below 30 percent hp an ally hangs back behind Ruth. At 0 hp it lies down for 20 s; hold E next to it (2 s, 1 med) to revive it with 30 hp, or "X is gone". Allies that live come into the managed phase with you. Ruth is not invulnerable in this phase: when she goes down she is dragged back to the breach.

Hook for other systems: `phase` on `prison_game.gd` ("clear", then "managed"), `in_managed()`, and the signal `phase_changed(new_phase)` (emitted after the managed base is built). Base attacks and the siege must run only when `phase == "managed"`. Flags: `--prison` boots "clear", `--managed` boots straight into "managed", `--auto=clearall` kills each zone in turn and searches one container in it (a check), `--auto=ally [--allyhp=N --at=x,y --walk=dx,dy]` lets the allies be bitten, `--log` prints zone and ally lines.

The two kids are now unarmed and play: `unarmed` in the person's def is read by `PawnNode` (no weapon drawn, in hand, put away or at work), and they have no route: one runs, one chases, a touch swaps them, a ball rolls along and a chalk hopscotch is drawn in the yard. They are freed with the yard.

Checked: boots into clear with the intro card and the party outside the breach (shot); `--auto=clearall` cleared 10/10 with real searches (stores rose with each container), each zone freed its people, then the phase switched, 17 members at work (14 crew and 3 allies), shot of the managed base; `--auto=ally` shows bites, down, revive (meds 3 to 0) and "gone". Not checked: a real human playthrough, so the 8 to 12 minute target is an estimate (99 dead, 32 containers, 10 rooms to walk), and the fight tuning (allies kill fast; losing one is likely only if you push into a big room first) needs a playtest.

## Siege (branch experiment/prison-siege)
`scripts/prison/prison_siege.gd`, owned by prison_game.gd and active only when `phase == "managed"` in the base. The fence is cut into 14 segments (north 4, south 4, west 3, east 3) plus the gate, 120 hp each (gate 160). A segment shows its state in the overlay: cracked under 66 percent, sagging under 33, down at 0 (the middle five tiles, the whole gate, become a gap with rubble and snapped posts), plus a small hp bar and a red pulse while it is being torn at; the minimap colours the fence line the same way. Nobody scripts the dead at the fence: the ones sent at a segment walk to a spot outside it, stand there, and each takes 0.5 hp a second off it; the pen's crowd leaning on the south fence takes a trickle.

Each night at 21:00 (bell at 20:30, three rings, banner such as "Night 3: the south fence and the north fence", the guards shout) a wave of 4 + 4 x night dead per segment is spawned in three batches outside 1 to 3 segments (1 on nights 1 and 2, 2 on nights 3 and 4, 3 from night 5; more runners each night). Nights 1 and 2 only choose segments a tower guard can reach, from night 3 at least one is out of reach, so the player has to go there. Guards shoot anything within 5 tiles outside the fence (2.2 by day) and everything inside, and every shot costs 0.25 rounds from the stores (0.05 by day). At dawn (06:30) or when the wave is dead a morning card shows fence lost, hp lost, dead killed of sent, ammo used, crew lost.

When a segment reaches 0 hp the tiles open for the real path finder, the real zombies in the pen near the gap and the wave walk in toward the nearest crew member or Ruth (they are slowed shufflers, wave dead move twice as fast), and Ruth can shoot, reload and melee in the base now. A crew member within 0.9 tiles of an inside dead one is bitten: "X is down", and after 6 seconds is removed ("X is gone") unless that dead one dies first. Repair: hold E within 3 tiles of a damaged segment, 8 hp a second, 1 scrap per 8 hp from the stores; Tomas takes the weakest segment as his next stop and mends 3 hp a second the same way. A gap shuts again at 25 percent hp if no dead stand in it. The HUD shows the weakest segment, the dead left and the next attack time.

Flags: `--siege=N` starts night N after the first frame, `--siegeseg=4,5` picks segments by index, `--noguards` switches the guards off, `--siegefor=S` ends the forced night after S seconds, prints one `SIEGE RESULT` line and quits, `--siegelog` prints the fence every 5 s, `--autorepair` holds E for a headless repair check, `--hour=22`. Needs a window (headless boots the old game): `Godot --path . --fixed-fps 60 --disable-vsync -- --prison --managed --siege=3 --siegefor=100`.

Fake or missing: crew do not flee, fight or sound the alarm; the downed pose is the existing one; nothing is saved; the dead cannot climb or tear the fence by day beyond the trickle; wave dead spawn outside the segment instead of walking from the pen across the map; the fence picture under a gap is covered, not removed; balance (0.5 hp a second, wave sizes) is one tuning pass.

## Round 6: rifles and rotation (branch experiment/prison-chars)
Rifles. The guards' "watch" work pose drew binoculars at the face (the tool of `WorkAnim._watch`), which is what the owner saw on the towers. In the prison the pose is no longer drawn: `PawnNode.rifles_ready` (set by prison_game.gd, off everywhere else, so the One House pawn tests and goldens are untouched) keeps a person whose work word is "watch" out of the work path and draws the ordinary armed pawn. Every armed person who is not aiming (tower and gate guards, patrol, allies, found survivors) carries the rifle at the low ready, eased in and out (`_rdy`, half way to the low carry), pointing the way the figure looks; a guard on watch slowly looks about (the heading drifts about 50 degrees either side of the post's facing, at 40 percent of the turn rate). When `target` is set the rifle is raised and aimed at the exact angle, the flash and tracer start at the barrel at that drawn angle. The rifle is a separate rotated sprite, so it is visible in every direction. Kids stay unarmed and workers keep their tools.

Rotation. The 4 baked views stay, but a person now keeps a continuous angle that turns at a fixed rate (`PawnMotion.turn_at`, no snap to a quarter): 220 deg/s walking, 130 deg/s while at a job or hauling, 360 deg/s with the gun up and for Ruth, 120 deg/s for the dead, 40 percent of the walk rate for the watch scan. The wanted heading is the aim when the gun is up (or Ruth), the drawn velocity when walking, and otherwise the rules' facing only when it changes (a stop keeps the angle it walked at). Across each seam between two views the next view is drawn over the nearest at up to half opacity (body and legs, 0.4 rad either side of the seam), so the figure turns through the in-between angles without the old narrowing; the weapon, its shoulder and the lean are continuous. Ruth: the aim is turned at 360 deg/s before it is given to the sim (`_aim_cur` in prison_game.gd `_hands`), so the drawn gun, the aim line and the shot all use the same angle. Aim mode (from the coordinator). For the companion command keys: `game.aiming_now` and `AimMode.holstered` are the two things to read; a right click must give an order only when `not aiming_now` (weapon away, or drawn with no right button held and a tap shorter than `AimMode.TAP_S`); the tap logic of the main game (scripts/main.gd `_tap_order`) was not copied because the orders are the other branch's.

## Round 8: zombie art set (branch experiment/prison-zart)
Why. The owner asked for much better zombies. Round 7 had two plain-walker looks. This round builds a rigged zombie in the same headless Blender pipeline (same orthographic camera at 16 degrees, same toon ramp, same 160 px to the tile, same ink line and part lines made by the packer) and a look and pose system on top of it, so the dead sit with the Round 7 bodies.

What was built. tools/make_zombie_dirs_blender.py holds one joint rig (fall, body, spine, neck, jaw, shoulders, elbows, hips, all empties) and builds the figure from a look dictionary: skin tone, garment style (shirt and tie, scrubs, prison jumpsuit, guard uniform, hoodie, cardigan, hi-vis vest, dress), torn sleeves and a ragged hem, bare forearms, an open chest with ribs, a bite on the shoulder, a torn cheek, blood stains and drips, hair or hat, a slack jaw on its own joint, uneven eyes (one big and low, one small and high, one clouded) in dark sockets, a head roll and a forward neck, a dropped shoulder, a dragged foot. Looks: 8 walkers (office worker, labourer with hard hat, nurse, prisoner, guard, kid in a hoodie, elder, woman in a dress), 4 runners, 4 brutes (broad shoulders, small head, big hands, rendered at 1.55 times the walker), 4 crawlers (legless torso that pulls itself along, with a ragged stump). No screamer or bloater (not cheap, and the rules have no such kind). The rules know three kinds (walker, runner, brute); the crawler is used for one in four limping walkers (PawnNode.zart_crawlers) because no kind of it exists, and its hit box stays the walker's.
Poses rendered (all 16 headings for the walk, 8 for the rest): walk (walker 8 frames with an irregular rhythm and a dragging foot, others 6), idle sway 4, attack 4 (rear back with the arms overhead, strike, slam, recover), hurt 2, forward fall 4, backward fall 3. What is in the atlases is smaller because of the web budget (packer table KEEP): walker walk 6 frames of the 8 at 16 headings, idle 2, attack 3, hurt 1, forward fall 3 (the last is the corpse); runner walk 6 at 16 headings, attack 2, hurt 1, fall 2; brute walk 4 frames at 8 headings, attack 2, hurt 1, fall 2; crawler walk 6 at 8 headings, attack 2, hurt 1, fall 2. The backward fall and the 8 frame walk are rendered but not packed: a shot zombie falls forward whichever way it was hit, and the irregular rhythm is carried by the 6 frames chosen and the dragged foot. The wake-up is the forward fall played backwards (4 frames at 8 headings were not affordable); it is drawn for 0.6 s when a zombie stops standing still.

Sizes. assets/zombies16: 20 palette PNG atlases (20 colours times 3 alpha steps), 11.4 MB in total (walkers 0.5 to 0.8 each, runners 0.3 to 0.4, brutes 0.5 to 0.6, crawlers 0.2 to 0.3; index.json 18 KB), under the 12 MB asked; the contact sheets docs/experiments/prison/zombies_*.png add about 5.3 MB to the repo. The Blender renders are not kept (about 12000 files). Imported with Godot's lossless mode they come to 32.4 MB (about 2.85 times the PNG, mipmaps on): that would be the growth of the web zip. The .import files of these atlases are therefore set to lossy WebP (compress/mode=1, quality 0.85, no mipmaps): 12.1 MB imported. In the game at 1x the lossy ones show no visible ringing on the ink line (compared in siege shots), but this was checked by eye on a desktop build only, not in a web export, and a 20 colour toon atlas is a kind that lossy compression handles worst at high zoom; if edges look soft in the browser, put those files back to mode 0 and drop mipmaps (about 24 MB). VRAM compression (S3TC, ETC2) is not safe for these: it would smear the thin ink lines and the alpha edge, and the web export would need both formats.

How it is used. scripts/render/pawn_dirs.gd (header and `zombie_*`): `PawnDirs.zombie_frame(kind, look, pose, phase, heading)` returns {tex, src, dst}; `zombie_look_of(kind, key)` picks a stable look from a key (the zombie's look_index, a stable per zombie number: same zombie, same look, also after a save); `zombie_heading(ori)` is the index 0..15 of PawnNode._ori; `zombie_blit` draws. Poses: walk and idle loop (phase in radians), attack, hurt, deathf, deathb, wake, corpse, corpseb are one-shots (phase is the progress 0 to 1); a pose a set does not have falls back (POSE_FALLBACK), heading is always an index of 16 and is snapped to the pose's own count. PawnNode (`zart`, `--nozart` to compare) takes a set per zombie in setup, and `_draw_zart` / `_draw_zart_dead` choose the pose from clocks the node already has: walk from the rules' walk_phase (the distance walked), idle sway from anim_t, attack from the windup and the age of the strike, hurt while the flinch clock runs, wake after state still ends, fall from `z.fall` (forward unless fall_dir is negative: with no backward set it is the forward one), corpse after. The facing and turn code (`_ori`, `_anim_dead`) is untouched; the old lean, roll, bob and squash of a walker are not applied on top, because the poses carry them. The old kind tint is not applied to these (the sprites have their colour), the size is the rules' size divided by BAKE_SIZE.

How to add a look or a kind. Add a dictionary to LOOKS in tools/make_zombie_dirs_blender.py (a kind needs an entry in KINDS and, for a new gait, a branch in walk_pose), render with `Blender --background --python tools/make_zombie_dirs_blender.py -- <outdir> <kind>_<n>` (about 4 minutes a set; run several at once), pack with `python3 tools/pack_zombie_dirs.py <outdir> <kind>_<n> --colors=20` (what is kept is the table KEEP), import with Godot, and set the .import to lossy as the others. `python3 tools/pack_zombie_dirs.py --sheets` redraws the sheets. A new pose is a function like attack_pose plus a row in pose_table and in POSES of the packer.

Check. Sheets: zombies_walker.png, zombies_runner.png, zombies_brute.png, zombies_crawler.png (every look at 16 or 8 headings and the walk cycle facing south), zombies_looks.png (the 8 walkers side by side), zombies_poses.png (idle, attack, hurt, fall strips at three headings), zombies_game_siege.png (four screenshots of a siege 3 crowd at the gate, `--cam=48.5,57,60 --seq=4,45`). Also seen in game: corpses after the guards fire, a raid start. Tests avatar 406, work_anim 25, pawn_kinds 17, pawn_mesh 10, dead1 12 pass (headless: the prison flag is not set there, so they do not draw these). `--perf` (managed, siege 3, no guards, 105 awake dead) on a loaded machine, in pairs: 9.7 ms with zart against 9.0 without, then 10.5 and 9.1 against 10.0 and 8.9 (the baseline of 6.3 was on a quiet machine, so only the difference is meaningful: about +0.3 to +0.7 ms, with the p95 noisy). Many looks mean many textures on the canvas, which breaks batching more than the two atlases of Round 7; the average stayed near the 8 ms line only on a quiet machine, not measured here.

Weak spots. Palette of 20 colours makes the toon ramp blotchy on pale clothes (white shirt, hard hat) and the lossy import adds a little more; the faces are a sphere with features: from the side the eyes are hard to read and the head is large on a hunched body; the crawler floats a little over its shadow (the live shadow is the standing one); the 6 of 8 walk frames lose some of the irregular rhythm; a backward fall and a wake-up of their own are missing; the attack is snapped to 8 headings and the idle too, so a standing one turns in 45 degree steps; brutes walk at 8 headings; the web size of the lossy import was not tested in a browser; the look of a figure that stands still and is seen from the back is mostly the back of a big head.
## Round 8: zombie motion and the spear bug (branch experiment/prison-zmove)
The spear. The tower guards, the gate guards, the patrol and the allies are crew made from the shared people file (assets/data/map.json), and `Crew._make` copied the person under the crew's `key` with their gun. Four of the crew (Sol, June, Ike and Hector) use the key "hector", whose gun is `pike`: a long thin spear picture, drawn by the same code that draws a rifle, so a guard on watch "held a spear and fired" with it. Rae, Mia, Nia, Bea and Ana use "ana" (a pistol), Marcus and Owen "marcus" (a hunting rifle), Walt and Cole "walt" (the service rifle), so the guards did not even share one weapon. Nothing draws a pike in the prison now, whatever the heading. Not re-rendered by hand for every heading after the fix: the rifle drawing is the one of rounds 6 and 7 that was already checked at every angle.

The dead, the pattern. Picked: steering as a thin layer over the rules, plus code-driven animation read from state flags (the skills' navigation and animation pages). Rejected: `NavigationAgent2D` (the game has its own grid path finder and rules, and its avoidance would fight the rules' crowd pass; one agent per dead one is not cheap at 105), a behaviour tree or a state machine per dead one (the rules already are the states, this only reads them), an `AnimationTree` (the dead are code-drawn sprites, there is no AnimationPlayer).

What was wrong. (1) Facing: the node only changed its angle when the drawn position moved more than 0.8 px in a step; a dead one that stood, leaned on a wall, was bitten into the fence or woke kept the rules' `facing`, which starts as "S" and only knows four values, so they looked down. Worse, the rules move a dead one without asking where its face points: a brute in the old run walked north while looking south (face against motion error 180 degrees, turn rate 0). (2) Gait: the legs' phase was the rules' phase, which is distance based, but the speed was constant, so a stride had no rhythm; a dead one that turned went on walking while the body swung round; a wave stacked at one spot of the fence because the crowd pass skips two that both stand still; nothing happened to a dead one that was woken (it just was there), a blow only gave a 0.3 s flinch, and a corpse fell sideways whichever way it was shot.

What was built. `scripts/prison/prison_dead.gd` (`ZMove`, stepped by prison_game.gd around each `sim.update`, off with `--nozmove`; the One House and the classic game never call it) and a flag `PawnNode.zmove` (set by the prison only). `Zombie` got a dozen plain fields that nothing else reads (heading, rise, pause, press, aim and so on). After each rules step: (a) a continuous heading turned at a limited rate, walker 1.9 rad/s, runner 6, brute 1.2 (each one has 0.8 to 1.25 of that), toward the next point of its path, the prey it is at, the fence it leans on or its slot at the wall; a dead one that stands looks slowly this way and that; the node reads it for both the 4 baked views (with the seam crossfade) and the 16-heading renders. (b) The step is cut by how far the heading is still off the way it wants to go, so it turns before it walks (walker stands still beyond 75 degrees, a brute beyond 57, a runner never below 25 percent of the step), and cut again by a lurch: the speed follows the stride (fast mid swing, slow as a foot lands; walkers 0.55 to 1.25 of the rules' speed, a limping one with a drag on every other step, runners an even 0.8 to 1.2, brutes a stamp 0.5 to 1.5), the phase of the legs is cut by the same share, so the feet never slide, and the legs' phase is `walk_phase` times a stride length by kind plus the dead one's own start. The averages stay at about 1 (walkers, runners) and 0.87 (brutes). (c) Pause: a wanderer that is not on an errand stops for 1 to 3.2 s now and then, sniffs, looks about; a sleeper that is told of something first holds 0.2 to 0.6 s for the head to come round (that is also the turn toward a noise before it moves). (d) Rise: a dead one that was asleep in the records and is woken gets up in 1.1 to 2 s (crouched, leaning, arms low, then standing) and does not walk before it is up. (e) Fence: the siege marks a dead one that has reached its spot (`press`, `aim`); it faces the wire, its arms are raised in the reach pose, and it claws on a beat of 0.9 to 1.8 s (the strike animation reads the `attack_t` the module resets). (f) Spread: two dead that both stand still and are nearer than 0.42 tiles are pushed apart (the rules' pass skips them). (g) The drawing: body surge on each swing and an uneven rock (a lurch), a runner pitches harder, a slump while it stands, the bite is a lunge of 0.16 tile along its exact heading (0.22 for a brute) followed by a recoil, a hit throws it back 0.16 tile and it stumbles for 0.6 s, a corpse falls the way the shot went (sideways on a shot along the screen, a squash and a slide toward or away from the camera on one across it). (h) Sound: `groan` events of the rules now play in the prison (they were silent before): a voice by kind (walker's near or, past 16 tiles, far file, a runner's growl a little higher, a brute's growl lower), four voices at most, faded by distance to Ruth over 34 tiles.
