# Prison management: first slice (spec, 2026-10-12)

Owner decision: build the first slice of base management in one go. Loot must matter: every raid answers a visible need, and every need is paid for with loot carried home. Nothing here is built yet; this file is the shared spec for the build agents.

## The loop

Raid, carry loot home, put it in the stash, spend it on projects at the project board, crew do the work, a building visibly changes, production and the prison's stage improve, daily upkeep and night sieges keep pushing back. The needs board at the gate shows the current shortage and which kind of place to raid for it.

## Resources

The base stock dictionary `stores` already holds food, water, ammo, meds and scrap. Add `parts` (electronics, pump and generator pieces) and `fuel`. Items in the backpack map to stock units through the existing item data; add item entries for parts and fuel cans. Storage has a cap per resource that rises with projects (larder, water tower). Food over the cap spoils, water over the cap is lost.

## Projects (data, `assets/data/prison_projects.json`)

Each building has up to three tiers. A project has: id, building, tier, cost (resource amounts), labor (crew-seconds of work at the site), required role, requires (other project tiers, power), and an effect. Cost numbers below are starting values for the tuning pass.

- Water: T1 rain barrels (scrap 10; a little water only while it rains). T2 hand pump on the well (scrap 20, parts 5; needs a worker). T3 electric pump and filter (scrap 20, parts 15; needs power tier 1 and fuel; runs with no worker). The water tower is a buffer and raises the water cap.
- Garden: T1 beds (scrap 8; crops grow on a cycle, a gardener plants and harvests). T2 irrigation (scrap 15, parts 6; needs water T2; faster growth). T3 greenhouse (scrap 30, parts 10; needs power 1; grows all year; fewer people needed).
- Kitchen: T1 stove (scrap 8). T2 larder (scrap 12; halves spoilage, raises the food cap). T3 canteen (scrap 20; meals feed more people per unit and give morale).
- Infirmary: T1 cots (scrap 8, beds +2; sick and bitten recover). T2 medicine bench (scrap 12, parts 4; meds treat bites). T3 surgery (parts 10).
- Dormitory: each tier raises bed capacity by 4 (scrap, parts). Bed capacity limits the population.
- Power: T1 generator (parts 12; burns 1 fuel a day). T2 floodlights on the towers and the fence (parts 8; night attacks are easier to see and guards hit more). 
- Fence: T1 patch every segment to full (scrap 15). T2 reinforced (+50 percent hp, scrap 30). T3 double fence (+100 percent hp, scrap 50, parts 10).
- Radio (Hub stage): T1 radio room (parts 20); later it will bring survivors and trade, but only the stage flag is built now.

Stages (shown on a card and in the HUD, and the prison looks different at each): Shelter (water T1, dormitory T1, a clean floor in the main rooms), Settlement (garden T2, kitchen T1, infirmary T1), Fortress (fence T2, power T2), Hub (radio T1 and every building at T2 or better).

## Production and upkeep

A game day is the existing day (6 minutes). Each person eats food 1 and drinks water 1 per day. Production per day: barrels 4 (rain only), hand pump 10, electric pump 24; beds 6 food, irrigated beds 12, greenhouse 20. A canteen adds 25 percent to the food value. The starting prison produces about 60 percent of what it consumes, so the first three days need loot, then projects close the gap. The HUD and the needs board show production against consumption per day, for example "Water +14 / -12". A shortage lowers morale; morale under 25 makes one person leave each night. Meals and clean rooms raise morale. Guards' ammo use is already charged from the stores. A power cut (no fuel) stops the electric pump and the lights; a fence breach can trample the garden (crop timer resets).

## Loot by place

Raid loot tables by place type: house (food, water, a little meds, scrap), shop (food, water), pharmacy and clinic (meds), hardware (scrap, parts), garage (parts, fuel, scrap), petrol station (fuel), police and army (ammo, rarely parts). Use the town generator's lot and building kinds and container kinds; where only containers and cars exist, tag by container kind. The needs board names the biggest shortage and the place type that fixes it; the raid map marks those places.

## People

Bed capacity caps the population. Rarely a survivor is found in a raid building; they join if they reach the extraction ring with you and a bed is free. Roles (gardener, cook, medic, mechanic, guard, cleaner, hauler) can be assigned in a roster panel; a project's labor is done only by someone with the required role, and a production building with no worker produces nothing (T3 pumps and greenhouse excepted). Unrest is the morale rule above.

## UI

Project board (E at the workshop bench): list by building, shows tier, cost, labor and what is missing; Start spends the cost from the stores and queues the work. Needs board (E at the gate, and a HUD line): shortages, per-day production against consumption, next attack, the suggested raid place. Roster panel (key R is taken: use B or a button on the board). Stage card when a stage is reached.

## Build split

1. Core (`scripts/prison/prison_econ.gd`, the data file, item data): resources and caps, projects and tiers, labor queue, daily production and upkeep, morale and leaving, stage rules, save-free state, a small API (`projects()`, `start(id)`, `tier(building)`, `daily()`, `needs()`). A text lab (`--econ-lab`) prints ten days with a scripted spender.
2. UI (`scripts/prison/prison_board.gd`): project board, needs board, roster, HUD line, stage card. Uses the core API.
3. World (`scripts/prison/prison_upgrades.gd`): the visible result of each tier in the compound (props, beds, garden beds, pump and barrels, generator and lights, fence look, cleaner floors), and crew doing the project work with the work poses; construction in progress shown.
4. Raids and people: loot tables by place, parts and fuel items, the needs-board raid marks, survivor finds and bed limit, and the 40 pack / 20 stores ammo start.

Merge order: core first (it defines the API), then the other three in parallel on branches cut from the merged core.

## Core as built (step 1, 2026-10-12)

Files: `assets/data/prison_projects.json` (every number: projects, caps, spoilage, per-person upkeep, production by tier, morale, stage rules, loot tables per place, crew-to-role map), `scripts/prison/prison_econ.gd` (the economy; a RefCounted made in `_ready` beside the siege, kept over raids, active only in phase "managed"), `items.json` (parts box and fuel can items). Pattern: plain data plus one owner object with its own signals; a Resource per project, an event-bus autoload and a state machine were rejected (the spec wants one JSON file to tune, there is one owner and four listeners, and a project's state is a word computed from built, labor done and requirements).

API (also in the file header): `projects(building="")`, `state(id)` (locked, available, queued, building, built), `can_start(id)` (ok, why, missing), `start(id)` (spends the cost from `stores`, queues the work), `tier(building)`, `stage()` (Camp, Shelter, Settlement, Fortress, Hub), `caps()`, `beds()` (used, cap, free), `daily_report()` (per resource stock, production, consumption, net, days left; morale; people; next shortage; what the last night lost), `needs()` (the biggest shortage and the place type that fixes it: food and water under three days of stock first, then what the next stage's projects still lack, resolved through the loot table `fixes`), `goal()` (project ids still missing for the next stage), `site(id)`, `add_labor(id, secs)`, `fence_mult()`, `hit_bonus()`. Signals: `project_done(id)`, `stage_changed(stage)`, `person_left(name)`, `shortage(resource)`.

Rules as built: the day changes at dawn (6:00). The tick adds production, takes 1 food and 1 water per person (all crew, kids included), loses 5 percent of the food to spoilage (half with the larder), clips to the caps, burns 1 fuel for the generator (no fuel means no power: the electric pump falls back to the hand pump and the floodlights go out), then updates morale and lets one person leave when morale is under 25 (never below 4 people; guards and kids last). Morale: shortage hits (food 40, water 50, times the unmet share, growing 60 percent per day in a row), worry when under three days of stock are left (up to 15), +3 a day when nothing is wrong, +kitchen and infirmary bonus while their worker is alive, -6 when people exceed beds. Production needs a living crew member of the building's role (gardener, hauler); labor for a project is done by the building's role too. Until the world agent calls `add_labor` from the crew's work stop, `fallback_labor` supplies it: each living worker of the role advances one queued project in game seconds. Crew stops no longer change food or water (`crew.econ_owned`); meds, ammo and scrap stops are unchanged. Fence: `siege.apply_fence(mult, patch)` rescales every segment's max hp when a fence tier is built (tier 1 mends all). Floodlights add 0.12 to the guards' hit chance at night while the generator runs.

Flags: `--econ=start:water2` builds that project (and the tiers below it) for free at the start; `--econ-lab[=days]` (use with `--managed`) runs the economy on its own clock, 30 game seconds a frame, prints one line a day and quits; `--econ-raid=0` turns the scripted raid off; `--log` prints one ECON line at each real day change. Run: `Godot --path . --fixed-fps 60 --disable-vsync -- --prison --managed --noguards --econ-lab=18`. The lab raid adds the loot of the place `needs()` names (loot tables in the JSON); the spender buys the cheapest project of the next stage's path (and anything else affordable once the path is empty, the dormitory only when few beds are free).

Tuned numbers that differ from the spec: start production food 10 and water 10 a day against 17 each (59 percent); tier totals food 10, 13, 18, 26 and water 10, 12, 16, 28 (the spec's "barrels 4, hand pump 10, electric 24" are totals per tier here, rain counted as 2 a day); costs about twice the spec for Settlement items and three times for Fortress (power 1 parts 30, power 2 parts 24, fence 2 scrap 70 and parts 6, water 2 scrap 35 and parts 10, garden 2 scrap 30 and parts 10, dormitory 1 scrap 18 and parts 5, radio parts 40); stores start with parts 2 and fuel 0; starting morale 45. Lab with a raid every day: Shelter on day 2, Settlement day 6, Fortress day 13, Hub day 19; without raids food is gone on day 6, the first person leaves on day 6 and five have left by day 11, nothing goes negative.

Open: the "clean floor" Shelter rule is not checked; `--auto=clearall` then managed starts from the clear phase's small stores (food 10, scrap 4), which the lab does not model; beds only count (the people side must enforce the limit); `person_left` takes the last non-guard crew member, which can be the well's hauler and halve the water.

## Raids and people as built (step 4, 2026-10-12)

Files: `assets/data/prison_raids.json` (every number: building kind to place type, minimum places, what one container of each place type holds, the gate's five options, the survivor pool of ten, roles), `scripts/prison/prison_loot.gd` (loot by place, raid marks, the gate's choice, `--loot-lab`), `scripts/prison/prison_people.gd` (survivors, recruits, people walking out). Hooks in prison_game.gd are marked `# RAIDS hook`; one line each in prison_crew.gd (state "leaving" is skipped) and prison_siege.gd (a recruit lost in a siege is forgotten). Patterns: loot is a seeded post-pass over the generated raid town (the generator is untouched; each container is re-rolled with a RandomNumberGenerator seeded by town seed and container index, so a seed always gives the same loot); the inventory is the existing data-driven grid (items.json), not a Resource per item.

Loot by place. A raid town is the small two-block town and has only 4 to 12 buildings, with no pharmacy, garage, petrol station or police post of its own. So each building kind is mapped to a place type (supermarket, butcher, cafe, diner and rowshop are shops, the kiosk a petrol station, the laundromat a garage, the barber a pharmacy, the agency a police post, the hardware store a hardware store, everything residential a house), and then a stable hash retags buildings of a surplus type until every type has its minimum (one each, two shops). When a town has too few buildings, one room of a house becomes the place. Container kinds narrow the roll (a fridge holds only food and water, a medicine cabinet only meds, a toolbox scrap and parts). Parts and fuel are the backpack items parts box (stack 2, one cell) and fuel can (one by two cells): they pass the loot window, the grid, extraction banking and the stash window, which now shows Parts and Fuel columns with withdraw buttons when the stock has them (the town game's stash is unchanged). Shared files touched, additively: items.json (`unit_items` parts and fuel, parts stack 2), scripts/sim/inventory.gd (`items_for_loot` also turns parts and fuel into items), scripts/town/inv.gd (`RES`), scripts/ui/inv_stash_window.gd, inv_ui.gd (deposit words), inv_icons.gd (two colours). Parts and fuel have no icon of their own yet: they draw as the generic box with a question mark.

Raid marks. At the gate, E opens a small choice (keys 1 to 5 or click; E or Enter takes the default, the option that fixes `econ.needs()`; Esc stays): food and water (shops), meds (pharmacies), parts and scrap (hardware, garages), fuel (petrol stations, garages), ammo (police posts). The chosen places get a coloured ring and a name with what they hold ("Pharmacy: meds") in the world, on the minimap and in the overview (M, where the rings grow so they can be read), and the raid HUD shows a "Marked:" line. Other places name themselves only when Ruth is within 16 tiles. The extraction rings are unchanged.

People. A survivor sits in a room of a building at least 22 tiles from the drop-off in about one raid in two (a hash of the seed), at most one. A ring, an exclamation mark and the bark "Help! Over here!" show when Ruth is near; E asks them to come. They follow with the clear phase's rules (a slot beside Ruth, a catch-up jump past 18 tiles, the dead nearer to them than to Ruth go for them, 8 damage per bite on 45 hit points; at zero they are lost and a banner says so). When Ruth reaches the extraction ring: close enough and a free bed (`econ.beds()`) gives the banner "Name joins." and a crew member; no bed gives "No free bed: Name is left outside."; too far behind gives "left outside" too. The prompt warns "(no free bed at the base!)" in advance. A new member takes the work role the base has fewest of (the role the biggest need points to wins ties), walks the same stops as the roster's person of that role (chat stops removed) and has one of ten names and looks; they are crew, so upkeep and morale count them, and `recruits` rebuilds them at every return to the base. When `econ` fires `person_left` the person is set to state "leaving", walks out of the gate and is removed on arrival, with a banner.

Flags: `--loot-lab=N` (N seeds, headless-fast, prints LOOTLAB lines), `--target=0..4` (mark a type without the choice), `--survivor` (force one; `=near`, `=run` scripted find and extraction, `=nobed` the same with a full base), `--mapview`, `--choice` (open the gate choice), `--carry` (parts and fuel in the pack), `--leave=Name` (fire `person_left`). Run windowed: `Godot --path . --fixed-fps 60 --disable-vsync -- --prison --managed --raid --seed=3 --survivor=run --frames=720`.

Known weak: the place a mark names is a building that does not look like it (a barber is the "Pharmacy"); a raid town with too few buildings marks a room, not a house; the survivor does not shoot; the lab's raid is a greedy walk, not a player; scrap runs a little above the 3 to 8 target.

## World as built (step 3, 2026-10-12)

Fence 1: patched segments (sheet metal and planks), 2: posts every two tiles, a second strand with barbs, 3: a second chain-link line a tile inside, tied to the first. Work on site. `update` hands every queued project to the living crew members of its role (the economy's `crew_role` map) as an `eat` stop of 24 seconds at a spot of the table `SITES` (a stand point, the prop, a face, a work pose and a scaffold size per project), only between 7:00 and 19:00 and never while a siege runs; a person who is walking to a route stop or a meal is not interrupted in the work itself, and meals come first. While the stop runs `labor()` calls `econ.add_labor(id, dt)` every frame, and `econ.fallback_labor` is false (except in `--econ-lab`). Poses: hammer (water, garden 2 and 3, kitchen 2 and 3, infirmary 1, dormitory, power 2, radio uses bench), bench (stove, power 1, medicine bench, surgery), garden (beds), mend (fence). A scaffold of poles and planks with a progress bar and the project's name stands at the site from the queue to the finish, grey when nobody is there; each blow raises dust and sparks and plays the hammer sound if the player is near; at the finish a ring of stars pops, "Built: ..." floats up and the confirm sound plays. Two workers on one site stand apart. If the role has nobody alive the project waits (no fallback).

Flags: `--tiers=water3,garden3,...` builds those projects and the tiers below at once, `--econ=build:all` builds every top tier, `--building=water2,fence1` starts those projects with the stores topped up (the tier below must be built, use `--tiers`), `--fuel=N` sets the fuel, `--noupg=all` hides everything and stops the file (a perf check), `--log` prints `WORLD stop` and `WORLD built` lines and any site a person cannot walk to. Screenshots: `docs/experiments/prison/upgrades_*.png` (before and after per building, all tiers, a night with the lights, a dirt pair, two scaffolds with the crew at work).

Weak: the crew walk far (a meal takes the medic about 40 seconds of walking each way, so a tier 1 project takes a few minutes of real time and a tier 3 with one worker much longer); props are placed by hand and walkable (the path finder does not know them); the dormitory shows only 6 of the 12 beds as bunks; the pump house and tower stand on the walk by the garden; the light cones are bluish under the night tint; tier 3 of the fence shows no extra drawing for a breached segment (the siege's marks only); the hum is a repeated one-shot, not a loop; nothing was run on the web build.

## Fix round A (2026-10-12)

Ten weak spots from the earlier steps, fixed at the root with small edits. Verified by windowed runs and logs; screenshots were read by eye, no clicks were driven (that is the other round's work).

1. Project pacing. A game hour is 15 real seconds (the day is 360 s). Labor is now 15, 38 and 75 seconds for tiers 1, 2 and 3 (power 1 is 38, radio 75, the numbers are in `assets/data/prison_projects.json`), and the new block `work` in the same file holds the 20 s stop length, the limit of two workers per project and Ruth's rate. A project takes the nearest free people of its role, at most two. A builder hurries (1.6 times walking speed) and skips the next meal when a project of his role waits, and eats at the one after (`busy_cb` in the crew). Ruth holds E at a scaffold and adds one second of labor per second (`_ruth` in prison_upgrades.gd). The board shows "about N h left" on the card and in the HUD line (`econ.eta_h`, the remaining labor over the number of workers on site). Measured: tier 1 about 1.1 game hours with two workers (water 1 at 8.0 built 9.1, infirmary 1 with the medic alone 8.0 to 9.3); tier 2 with two workers about 1.9 (hand pump 9.0 to 10.9); tier 3 with the cook alone 5.4 (kitchen 3, 7.0 to 12.4). The lab (`--econ-lab=18`) is unchanged because it counts labor in big steps and loot is its limit: Shelter day 2, Settlement day 6, Fortress day 13, Hub day 19.
2. Props block. Every prop of a built tier shuts its tiles in the walking and dead grids and in both path finders (`_block_props`, run at the start and at every `project_done`): barrels, well, pump house, tower legs, generator shed, tank, radio desk and mast, cots, infirmary bench and surgery, range, larder shelves and crates, the benches of the canteen, and every bunk. Shots still pass (the `shot_low` grid). A route stop that lies in a shut tile is moved to the nearest open tile (`_where` in the crew), and the radio's work spot moved south of the desk. `--econ=build:all --log` prints a walk test: 74 points (sites, route stops, stations), 0 shut, 0 not reachable from the gate, 135 prop tiles shut. The greenhouse is a roof over beds that are objects already: it is not walled, because the gardeners work under it.
3. Dormitory. One bunk per bed a tier adds: four a tier, twelve in all, between the cell doors in the corridors (block A north row, block A south row, block B north row). The HUD beds number is the 18 cell beds plus four a tier, so the bunks drawn match what a tier adds.
4. Generator hum. A one second generated loop (60 Hz and harmonics, whole cycles) on an `AudioStreamPlayer2D` at the shed, looped in the stream resource, -24 dB, falling off over 18 tiles, stopped when there is no power or no fuel. Pattern: spatial player with a looping stream (audio-system skill); the old repeated one-shot is gone.
5. Floodlight cones. They were an additive layer under the night tint, so the tint made them blue. They are drawn in the game's glow layer, above the tint (`draw_cones`), warm white. The same fix corrected the glow list: the tower, pole, shed and table glows were given in pixels where the layer wants tiles, so they never showed. Checked at 21:00 and 03:00.
6. Build effects. Seen on screen: scaffold with the progress bar and the name (the label was cut off, now wider), the worker in the hammer pose at the site, dust and sparks on each blow, the pop ring and "Built: Hand pump on the well" (the floating text was cut off, now wider) with the toast. The earlier shot without a worker was a worker still on the way.
7. Role changes. The roster moves the person: a new route of the role (gardener to the garden, medic to the infirmary, cook, mechanic, hauler, cleaner to a sweeping route and the cleaning rounds); a guard goes to a free one of the six tower posts and stands there; a tower guard moved to another role comes down and walks the new route. Refused with a toast: during an attack (every role is locked then), and when all six posts are manned ("All 6 tower posts are manned"). After a raid the rebuilt crew is moved to the roster's roles again (`sync_roles`). Staging: `--role=dez:gardener,marcus:guard` at frame 40.

## Weapons, companions and scavenging (comp round, branch experiment/prison-comp)

Owner feedback this answers: start with an SMG or a rifle and not always the shotgun; bringing people on a raid was unclear; scavenging was weird, someone you bring should be able to clear a house, bag the loot and follow you.

Guns. Three guns, all in `assets/data/prison_weapons.json`, which is read at start-up and laid over the shared game data of the prison process only (`prison_arms.gd` `merge`), so the One House game, its data and its goldens are untouched. The pump shotgun keeps its numbers (6 shells, 1.05 s, five pellets, heard 16 tiles). The service rifle is the town's existing M1 (wooden picture, 8 round magazine) made semi-auto in feel: 0.6 s between shots, a tight 0.04 cone, reach 17, heard 14 tiles. The SMG is new: 30 rounds, a shot every 0.1 s, 0.7 damage a round, a wide 0.16 cone that grows while the trigger is held, reach 9.5, heard 13 tiles, the pistol sound at a higher pitch and a lower level, and its own picture (`tools/make_weapon_art.py` draws `assets/weapons/smg.png`, same size and outline as the other guns). Ammo is one stock for every gun; a gun's `cost` says how much of a unit a round takes (shotgun 0.34, rifle 0.25, SMG 0.1: 3, 4 and 10 rounds to a unit), so a full SMG magazine is 3 units, a full rifle one 2, a full shotgun one 2. This was chosen over an ammo stock for each gun because the stores, the stash window, the economy lab and the loot lab all count one `ammo` and stay as they were; the lab runs unchanged. The sim has three small hooks that read a gun's own keys when it has them and do nothing when it has not: `shot_cost` reads `cost`, the noise rule that wakes the dead (`dormant_dead.gd` `noise_radius_for`, the dead within the radius come to look) reads `noise`, and the avatar's shot event carries `pitch` and `vol` for the sound. Items `gun_shotgun`, `gun_service` and `gun_smg` are in `items.json` (one to a place, no store, pictures drawn in `tools/make_item_icons.py`); the backpack's weapon slot shows the gun in her hands.

The weapon rack. The base owns a count for each gun (`armory`, starts with one of each). At the start of the clear phase a card asks which gun to take.
