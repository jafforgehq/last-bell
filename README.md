# Last Bell (Godot)

> **The prison (2026-10-15, playtest-2 build, the game on `main`).** You play Ruth, a survivor who breaks into a zombie-overrun prison with five allies (Cole, Bea, Ike, Hal and Wren) and clears it, six zones of dormant dead (71 to 73 dead in a compound of 57 x 50 tiles), with a shotgun, a rifle or an SMG. Every door between the sections starts closed (E opens or shuts it) and all open once the last zone is clear. Then the managed phase: you run the prison as a base for 30 days. The Base screen (B, or the Base (B) button at the bottom) has five tabs, each with a one-line explanation: Projects (ten projects in nine buildings, each with a plain line on what it gives and a status such as "Missing 12 scrap" or "Needs a hauler"), Needs, Roster (who does which job), Defence and Story (L); a first-day checklist on the left walks through opening it, starting Water (the clear always leaves at least the 20 scrap it costs; if the scrap is spent first, the step reads "Raid for scrap (you need N more)" and points at the truck), giving a job, posting a guard and waiting for the night. It holds production, upkeep and morale; a cleaner job keeps the trash down (trash spoils food and drags morale); crew posts and barricades (Base, Defence tab) decide who holds which part of the 14-segment fence at night. From the gate you take raids into four kinds of town (industrial, residential, hospital, police) to loot and to rescue survivors, the only way to get people. The base is attacked only on horde nights, every three to five days (announced the day before; the top bar says "Quiet night, next horde: night N"), by hordes with special infected (screamer, bloater, hunter, breacher). Morning event cards, six prison traits with short bios, difficulty presets (Easy, Standard, Hard) and a real run seed. On day 30 a convoy arrives; the endings depend on how the base and the convoy's parts and fuel list stand. Saving: autosave at dawn and when a raid returns, Continue on the title, Save and Save and quit in the pause menu (not in a raid, a night attack or the clear phase). Left click fires by default; F1 to F3 set base speed. A Copy report button in the pause menu puts the playlog on the clipboard for testers.
>

Flags: `--prison`, `--managed` (skip the clear phase), `--day=N`, `--seed=N`, `--savefile=NAME` (keep a test off the real save), `--nodoors`, `--posts=`, `--barricade=ID`, `--barricades-now=`, `--onehouse` (the older One House game). Tests: `Godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/run.gd` (about 4400 checks; add `-- --only=NAME` for one file; a fresh checkout needs `--headless --path . --import` first). Web build: `Godot --headless --path . --export-release "Web" build/web/index.html`. The current state and open items are in `STATUS.md`; the Steam readiness audit is `docs/audit/2026-10-13-steam-audit.md`. No paid art or AI-generation services are used: sprites are code-drawn or made with PIL scripts. Not yet done: no human has played all 30 days, no desktop build or Steam art, no controller support or key rebinding.


> **One House (2026-10-05, now the game on `main`).** New game starts ONE survivor alone in ONE house of a big generated town (192 x 192 tiles: districts, irregular streets, many house shapes). Roofs fade as you step in; rooms you have not opened are dim. Clear every room, search, **claim** the house (a stash of scrap is in it), **build the front fence** yourself (order it on the Build page, N, then stand at it, press E once and stay by it), **rescue** survivors (they join, take jobs and answer the radio and the command wheel); when the **second** is home the dead come every night and you fortify the house with a few **projects** on the Build page (the front fence, the gate, a watchtower, the side and back fences, floodlights, door planks, reinforcing the fences: the page says which is next, one button orders a whole project, and "Build everything I can afford" orders what the scrap pays for); hold the first attack to reach the end card (about 20 to 30 minutes). Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-04-one-house-design.md`; plan and contracts: `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-04-one-house-plan.md`; foundation numbers: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-04-bigmap-spike-report.md`. Flags: `--town[=seed]` (the default for a new game), `--town-blocks=2` (a 64 x 64 mini town), `--stage=cleared|claimed|rescued1|rescued2|fourth|night` (`rescued2` is the second voice, which begins the attacks; `fourth` is the same a day later: the night before the first attack's day), `--hands=idle|hold[:F]|done|up` (after `--stage=claimed`: the front fence ordered and her hands at it, E held F of the way, or the fence up at once), `--rescue=r0[:clear][:come|:stay|:gift]`, `--slots=built|damaged|sites|ring|all|stress` (after `--stage=fourth`; `--radio=build,focus:front` opens the Build page with a project picked), `--school` / `--demo` (the earlier school-yard game and its three-night demo, kept behind flags), `--town-spike` (the old standalone town scene). Lab: `Godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/house_lab.gd -- --seeds=1..8 --mode=bot,idle --jobs=4` plays the whole arc headless and prints milestones and verdicts (`--scavengers=N` puts N of the crew on the Scavenger job from the claim, `--scavhouses=K` also clears the K houses nearest the base for them; PH5: `--nights=N` plays to the dawn that makes N attack nights held, the data's `end.nights_to_hold` (1, the first attack night, as it was; a night not held does not count and the run goes on), and prints one line for each night and the summary tables, `--daywork` makes the bot go on rescuing and searching on the days of the attacks); `tools/town_overview.gd` draws the whole town as one image (a thin frame round each candidate is its field of fire: 10 tiles of open ground round the lot, no other building and nothing tall); `tools/town_probe.gd -- --seeds=1..64 --twice` makes the towns of many seeds and checks each (three candidates in range, everything reachable, clear fields, the same seed the same town). **The New game seed is the town's seed**: the same number is the same town, Random a new one. The text below describes the school-yard game and its systems, which the town reuses (radio, wheel, jobs, nights, building).

A small crew holds a walled schoolyard against the dead for thirty nights: five survivors to begin with (three if you choose), one of whom you play, and more who call in over the radio. Built in Godot 4.7. (The name was Thirty Bells for a while; the owner chose Last Bell again on 2026-10-07. A Steam game called The Last Bell exists: check before any public release. The save folder keeps its old name ThirtyBells so saves survive.)

Open the project and press **F5**. A start with a run choice on the command line (`--new=1`, `--seed=N`, `--crew=3`, `--load=path`, `--shot=...`) goes straight into the yard.

### The school yard (`--school`, `--demo`) and the notes of the avatar game

The rows below describe the school-yard game and its systems, which the town reuses. Where they talk of picking someone, the dock, a tapped right click, the middle button, 1 to 5, Tab, Y or B, that is the school's: the town has none of them (see the table above).

**Commanding the crew in the One House: one way.** You never "pick" anyone while you walk, aim and shoot: there is no selected person, no dock, no selection ring, no Tab, no 1 to 5 and no right-click order. To ask somebody for something, **click them** (their picture in the world, or their **card** in the crew bar) and **the order wheel opens round them**; it works by radio, from any distance, and the person is named in the middle with how they are (*steady*, *following*, *down*). Pick a slice with a click: *Come with me*, *Hold here*, *Cover me*, *Search a house* (the crew search for you: see below), *Go back*, *To the walls*. *Hold here* needs a spot: the wheel goes away, a note at the pointer says **"Hold here: click where. Esc: cancel"**, and the next click on the ground puts it down (a flag marks it for a few seconds); *Search a house* is the same with **"Search a house: click a cleared house. Esc: cancel"**; every other slice asks at once. They answer in a toast ("Mei: on my way"); when they say no, the wheel stays up with the reason in amber and that slice now reads **Insist**: click it to push them (it costs trust), or ask for something else. A slice greyed with its reason is a refusal nobody can argue with (down, in a breakdown, leaving). The **middle** of the ring is a word with them (what they need; the first word of each day earns trust). **Esc** or a click outside the ring closes it. One accelerator: hold **C** for the wheel of the *nearest* crew member (release over a slice to pick it; a tap leaves it up for a click), **Shift + C** for everyone within eight tiles (one ask each, one line of answers); **Z** asks the last order again of the same people, to the same spot. **E** at a person is a click on them. Clicking a dead one, or the bare ground, does nothing special; a person who is hiding in a house (not yet yours) still has the rescue card. The crew bar is status only (health, what they do: *Following*, *Holding*...) and a click on a card opens the wheel. The speeds are **Shift + 1 2 3** (the plain digits 1 to 4 use the quick slots on Ruth's belt). Jobs, the Map and the News stay on the radio panel (**T**). *The rows below that talk of picking someone, the dock, a tapped right click, the middle button, 1 to 5 or Tab are the school yard's (`--school`, `--demo`).*

**The crew search the houses (One House, `scripts/town/crew_search.gd`).** Two ways to make looting easier once there are people at the base. **The order**, *Search a house* on the wheel: choose it, click a house that is **cleared**, and they walk to its front door, go in, search every container nobody has searched, nearest first (4 s each, standing at the container), and the loot goes straight to the stores (the pops fly over the container as for you; "Femi searched 22 Elm St: 4 food, 2 scrap" is a toast and in the News); then they are back to their job. **The job**, *Scavenger* on the Jobs page (two seats, from the claim; nobody is put there by Auto-assign or Fill empty seats): they take the nearest cleared house to the base (within 35 tiles) that still has something, search it fully, then the next, at any hour and in any weather; with nothing left their line says **"Nothing left to search: clear another house"**, and the row says what they have found today. The morning summary adds "Scavengers found: 12 food, 6 scrap yesterday". **An order is obeyed (RULES2: "when I say someone to scavenge a house he must do that, it is not optional").** Nothing about the weather (rain, a storm), the hour (dusk, dark, the watch), the person (hurt, bleeding, ill, shaken, tired, their trust), the distance, the dead near the door or the number already out is a reason to say no, and nothing of it ends a run: a person goes, searches every container and comes back when the house is done, or is down. The only "no" is for what cannot be done, said in the toast with its reason: *Click a house*, *Not cleared yet: there are dead in it*, *Already searched* (or somebody is on the last containers), *No way in*, and a person who is down, gone, away, in a breakdown or the one you play. A person outside is a person: the dead near them wake (a smaller circle than yours), they shoot what they see, and bites are counted but nobody turns back for one (deaths are real). A run is taken back only by you (a new order, *Go back*, the call to arms). The one thing the JOB still yields to is the base's night posts (the call to arms or an attack night take every hand to the wall, and no job run starts then); an order keeps its person out through those too. They make no noise searching. **The cars (PH3 SCAV3, `scripts/town/car_loot.gd`; the owner: "the scavenger should also check for cars automatically and loot them").** The parked cars and wrecks of the streets are containers now: you search one with E from outside ("Search the car", 2.5 s, a gold shimmer on every unsearched one within ten tiles while you are outdoors, a green tick over a searched one), and a car cannot be stripped for scrap before it is searched, like a fridge. About three in ten hold something, one or two items (ammo, food, water, a med kit, a bit of scrap: the `cars` block of `assets/data/salvage.json` has every number, the roll is made once per car from the town's seed). On the order *and* on the job, after the last container of the house the trip goes on to the cars and wrecks within 14 tiles of it, nearest first, up to six: each is searched (the avatar's own 2.5 s) and then stripped for scrap too when salvage allows (the street cell cap, no awake dead within 15 tiles, not the base's); a car you have already searched is not searched again (stripped only), and a save keeps it. With every house done, the job takes a trip of its own from the car nearest the base. Loot comes before strip work all through. The toast says what the cars gave ("Femi searched 22 Elm St: 4 food, 2 scrap; 2 cars: 6 ammo, 1 water"). A save keeps the runs and the day's tally.

**How to play the avatar game.** A new game is played by **one survivor** (Ruth to begin with), seen from above: the camera follows them, and the mouse is a free arrow that clicks: the survivor faces the way they walk (and keeps their last facing when they stand still), and only while you **hold the right button** (or Shift) does the arrow become a small crosshair, the survivor turn to it and a thin line from the gun show where a shot would go (it fades with the distance and goes while the gun is reloading). A left click never fires and never turns the survivor unless the right button is down: it picks a person, or uses what the prompt is on. If you would rather have the old way (the survivor aims at the mouse all the time and the left button fires), choose **Always aim at the mouse** under *Controls* in Settings. **Q puts the weapon away** (and draws it again; so does the small *Weapon: drawn (Q)* button under the gun's readout): nothing aims or fires, the pointer is the free arrow whatever the setting says, and the survivor carries the gun low. Then, with someone picked (click them, or their crew card), **a right click on the world is an order by radio** for them: on the ground *Hold here* at that spot (a small flag marks it for a few seconds), on a night post *To the walls* at that post, on a person *Come with me*; the answer, a refusal's reason and *Insist* are the dock's own. **Commands are even easier with the command wheel** (round 24): hold **C** (or the middle button) and a ring of the radio's six verbs opens round the pointer; release over a slice and the person it is for is asked, exactly as the dock's buttons ask (see the table). The other survivors have their own minds and fight from their posts. There is no box select and no panning; the clock's column has a readout of the gun (the weapon, the rounds you can fire over the rounds in the stores (shots come from the stores, so a magazine over one round in the stores reads 1 / 1), amber when the magazine is low or the stores cannot fill it, red when they are dry, and a bar that is the reload). A saved game keeps its survivor; a save made before the avatar (or a Full crew save, which had none) loads as an avatar game with Ruth, or the first of the living if she is gone, as the survivor, and a toast says "Old save: you are now Ruth" (an old Full crew save has no town, so its Map page says "This old save has no town: start a new game to take trips"). There is only this game: Orientation Day is taught with the avatar, in the controls above (eight steps, from the YOU tag to the gate and the bell; Skip is on every card).

## Playing

A new game starts on day 1 at 07:00 with a quiet yard. The dead come at dusk, more each night. *Where this section says select, right-click or names the keys M, B, P, C or X, it means the order as the radio panel asks it of the person it is for (the order keys of the old yard are gone; see "The avatar game" below).*

- **Supplies.** At 07:00, 13:00 and 19:00 everyone eats and drinks. The stores panel shows how many days of food and water are left. The rain barrels give a little each dawn, and the vegetable beds grow food by the hour for whoever works them. Repairs cost scrap (1 for every 10 points of wall health), bandages cost medicine, and shooting costs ammo: when the stock cannot pay for another shot the guns stay silent and people fight with the butt of the gun (a pike never runs dry), and an alert says so.
- **Wounds.** A bite or scratch bleeds until someone bandages it (Ana first); at 0 health a person is downed and must be stabilised within three game hours, then rests in bed. A **bite** can infect: a feverish person turns into a walker 12 to 24 hours later unless treated (**T**, two medicine, better with Medicine skill) or sent out through the gate (**X**).
- **Scavenging.**

  The world beyond the wall is the **Town Board**: press **G** (or the bag in the toolbar) and a paper map of the town opens, with a pin for each place you know (an icon for what kind of place it is; a tick when it has been cleared, a flag when it is your outpost, a red disc with a number for a nest, a small figure for someone hiding there) and a faint question mark for each place nobody has seen yet. Click a pin, or Tab and the arrows to it and press Enter: the card says how dangerous the place is, how long the trip takes (a trip that would run past dusk is "back tomorrow": a night out, with nobody on the wall), what it is likely to give, and what is known about it. Pick one to three people who are free (the leader first; a lone scavenger brings back less, a good leader finds more) and one of six things, each of which says why it cannot be done when it cannot: **Go** searches the place for loot; **Scout** looks the way over and shows the places near it, with nothing brought back; **Clear** turns the dead out of the place and ends a nest (a nest needs two people; the page tells the chance in words, from the party's Shooting against the place's danger); **Claim** makes a cleared place with an outpost yours, for scrap, and it pays a daily yield at dawn; **Garrison** leaves the party at an outpost to hold it against the night (an outpost with nobody there can be overrun) until **Recall** brings them home. The map shows each party that is out on its road, there and back, and the strip under it says who is out, where, and when they are due ("back about 14:30", "back tomorrow"). A nest sends the dead to the night: clear it and the heading it leans to quietens and the night shrinks a little. A tip comes once, the first time the board could be opened. With an avatar the same map is the **Map page** of the radio panel (**G**): the people as chips, the two actions (Take control, Ask to go) and the trip's terms; **Shift+G**, or the page's **Full page** button, opens the Town Board above in full.

  **Vehicles and fuel (round 13, a small start only).** The town holds a few vehicles (a sedan, a pickup, a van, a bus) in its places: a place that has one says so on its card ("A pickup stands here: loot it to try to get it running"), and a party that loots or clears it and comes home may have got it running and driven it home. It is then the colony's: it is **parked at the gate**, in the north street, in the picture of its kind (up to four stand there; one that is out with a party is not drawn, a badly worn one is darker and a nearly done one has a small spanner over it), and a click on one opens the Town Board. Once the colony has a vehicle the party picker has a **ride** row under the people: **On foot**, or a chip for each parked vehicle with its name, its seats, the **fuel** the trip to the chosen place burns and the trip's hours by it; the one you pick sets how many may go (its seats in place of three), the hours on the card and in the buttons' tips, and goes with **Go**, **Scout** and **Clear**. A vehicle makes the road in about half the time, carries more loot and burns fuel for each hour driven; what the rules refuse (not enough fuel, too many people for the seats) is written under the buttons like the other reasons. **Fuel** is found as loot (the gas stations, the garage, the truck stop, the fuel depot across the river) and kept in a tank of its own: in a small start the stores panel shows a **Fuel** line (a pump) once there is a vehicle or any fuel, in amber while it is under ten and there is a vehicle to burn it. **Across the river** the city (ten places, from a hospital and a mall to a rail yard and a power station) is too far to walk to: its pins have a blue ring of dashes and the card says "By vehicle only". The map shows what is known, not the whole board: it is the town at the start, and it widens when the bridge is found (the river is drawn through it and a chip at the east edge says "Across the river") and again when the city is. A party that went by vehicle is a blue plate with a small car on it, and the strip at the foot says "in the van". A tip comes once, the first time the colony has a vehicle, on the toolbar's Town Board button.
- **A played trip: taking control of the squad.** A trip does not have to be left to the dice: the party can be played on a small map of the place itself (scripts/raid/: `raid_run.gd` the rules, `site_gen.gd` the places, `raid_scene.gd` the scene). The squad arrives at the road's edge (or at its vehicle), the dead stand about the place and notice the squad within seven tiles and in sight (a shot is heard from twelve), and the things to search (shelves, crates, cabinets, fridges, lockers, trunks, pumps) hold what the place is known for. Search by standing at a container for a few seconds; the bags hold thirty for each person standing, and a vehicle's cargo room more; get everyone back to the road to leave. What is brought home, who is hurt (bitten) or lost, whether the place was cleared, whether someone was found and the ammo used are the trip's result, exactly as the dice would have made it. **The base goes on while you are out** (round 17): taking control asks the people you picked over the radio to come (whoever cannot is left out and the road's overlay and the report say why, in amber; the survivor you play always goes and leads), the colony people vanish from the base, the road there is a live drive (round 21, below; the old full-window overlay, "Walking to the pharmacy...", a bar with the base's clock and the news that comes in, remains for `--fastroad`, the old yard and the tests), and in the place the base runs behind you at one step in three, so a second of the place is a minute of the day and a night can fall while you are out (the place is lit by the base's hour; Space pauses both). The way out is walking into the exit with everyone alive, after you have left it once (the squad follows you; **All back** calls the ones who are elsewhere); the road home is driven the same way and a report over the base's view (loot, who is hurt or lost, the news that came in) ends it. Controls: the second table above (**E** searches the container in reach, or gets a friend who is down up; E again stops); the camera follows you and leans toward the pointer. There is no selecting, no box, no right-click order and no panning: the squad is asked over the **radio** panel at the right (click a card, or a name under "At the base", then a verb: Come with me, Hold here at where you stand, Cover me, Search that (what the pointer is on), Go back (to the exit); the people at the base can only be asked to go back or to the walls). The panel also has **News** (the log, and the calls that wait for an answer, which never pause anything; the oldest is a card above the gun), **Alarm** (the call to arms at the base) and **All back**. When you fall the next of the squad leads (the camera glides, a banner says who); the base is told when the trip ends, and only if you are lost then (everyone trusts the player ten less and the base mourns); if you were got up and came home it only has a new survivor to play, with no setback. The game does not save while a trip is out (the pause menu's Save says "Not while you are out"), and a story card that came in while you were out waits until the report is dismissed, then holds the game until it is answered. The cards along the top show each person's health, weapon and magazine and what they are doing (You, Following, Holding, Covering, Searching, Down...), the bags' tally is at the top right, the gun (the magazine and the stores, a reload bar) at the foot, the base's day and hour and how it stands (walls holding, under attack, alarm) at the foot right, the last four lines of what happened fade at the foot left, and a banner says when the dead have heard a shot or a friend has fallen. The Town Board's "Take control" button, and the Map page's, open it (`main.open_raid(people, place, vehicle_id, loot_mult, on_done)`; the flow's rules are `scripts/raid/place_flow.gd`).
- **Patrol (One House, PH3; `scripts/town/patrol.gd`).** In the town nobody has to be moved about while the dead are at the fence. A person with no post is **on patrol**, the default for the whole crew, and the Duty roster's *Night post* column says **Patrol**. While the posts are manned (the call to arms, an attack night, a day under attack) they walk the loop of the base's fence by themselves, about a tile and a half behind it (corners and the gate included), at a calm pace, always the same way round; each takes a stretch and they keep apart; at a corner they stop and look from one fence to the next, now and then they stop to look over the fence. When the dead press a stretch the nearest free patroller goes there, stops and shoots (the colony's own shooting and ammo rules), and goes back to the walk when it is quiet. Where the pressure is: the dead near each point (clawing, breaching, or coming up to the fence, within about 12 tiles) plus the sections that are worn, and most of all a fallen section (a gap: they gather behind it); a second patroller joins only where the pressure can be shared, they never take points less than 2 tiles apart, a patroller stays at least 2 s at a point of pressure, and goes to another only if it is worth much more (no jitter between two targets). They never leave the lot: one who is outside (a rescue run) walks back to the loop. A person **given a post** (a tower, a stretch of fence: the roster's list, the radio's *To the walls*, E at a post) holds it exactly as before; the roster's post list has **Patrol** at its top to take them off it. Builders, scavengers, the radio desk and the medic keep their jobs by day and the call to arms recalls them to the patrol as it recalled them to their posts. The avatar is not on patrol. Numbers: the `patrol` block of `assets/data/one_house.json` (defaults in `patrol.gd` `KNOBS`; `tools/patrol_probe.gd` logs a night's walk).
- **Jobs and stations (J).** Everyone has a day job and a night post, and you ask for them on the **Jobs page** of the radio panel (**J**, where a job or a post is *asked*; **Shift+J** opens the full **Jobs screen**, the duty roster): a table of the crew with a day job and a night post to choose for each, the six stations with their seats and settings, and **Auto-assign** (fills the seats by skill) and **Clear**. The game waits while it is open; hovering a row, a station or a post marks its place in the yard. By day a person works at their station, at the bell they go to their post and hold it, and a person with nothing to do mends a badly worn wall (under half) or stands at ease. (In a small start, round 16, the crew works by day and is off the walls: the Builders sort materials at the builders' yard in the south-west corner when there is nothing to build or mend, a person with no job rests at a picnic table, and the posts are held only from the bell, and by day when the dead come to the wall, until ten game minutes after the last of them; the lookout stays at the tower.) An order (the radio's *Hold here*, *To the walls*, *Come with me*) beats the job until you release it, then they go back to it. The stations:
  - **Workbench** (2 seats, Building): turns scrap into ammo, 8 ammo for each scrap (2 scrap an hour, plus half a scrap for each point of Building), while the ammo is under the station's target (300) and the scrap above its reserve (3). **M** gives the selected person this job or takes it away.
  - **Builders** (3 seats, Building): mends the most worn wall section under the station's threshold (70%) while there is scrap, and builds what you order on the Build screen (below); a repair mends 5 points a second plus the person's Building skill. **B** gives or takes the job. **F** is still a one-off repair order.
  - **Vegetable beds** (2 seats): the beds grow about 0.2 food an hour for each gardener on top of a small share that grows unattended; one gardener is worth about 3 food a day, two about 5.
  - **Infirmary** (2 seats, Medicine): the people here treat the hurt first, and patients resting in a bed heal half as fast again while someone is on duty.
  - **Radio desk** (1 seat, Leading): the day's news at noon comes only if someone is at the desk at 12:00, and that person speaks. With nobody there the radio is silent that day and an alert says so; the one exception is a **survivor's call** from one of the first callers (Ana, Walt or Hector, whoever is still to call): it comes through even with the desk empty (the card says "The desk was empty, but the set had been left on"), so a crew whose desk is empty by luck is not stuck without recruits. A stranger's call, and every other card, still need someone at the desk.
  - **Lookout post** (1 seat, Shooting): the 17:00 forecast comes only from someone on watch there. With nobody there nobody tells the crew, and an alert says so.
  Ammo is what runs out first, and scrap is what the walls, the bench and the scrapyard trips are all about.
- **The crew-load strip, and what a trip leaves at home (round 16).** Under the stores, at the top left, a small slate stamp reads **Seats 5 of 11**: how many of the colony's job seats have someone in them (assigned to the station, here and up: not away on a trip, not down; only the stations the colony has count, so the salvage bench's two seats only once it stands, and the workshop and the greenhouse add theirs). Its tooltip lists the empty seats in the order of what they cost you (the radio desk and the lookout post first, then the builders, the workbench, the infirmary, the beds) with the price of each (\"no forecast at dusk\", \"walls and building wait\", \"no ammo is made\"...), and says so when the next survivor will still call with the desk empty. It is calm while the radio desk and the lookout post are staffed and amber (the edge and the words, never red) when one is empty; a click opens the Duty roster (J). It follows people as they leave on a trip (from the moment they set off for the gate) and as they come back (`scripts/sim/crew_load.gd`). It is in the settlement game only: the classic start's stores column is as tight as the buttons beside it were laid out for, so it has no strip. On the **Town Board**, under the party, an amber line says what the trip leaves empty at home (\"This leaves the Radio desk and the Workbench empty.\") in the same order and, when two or fewer would be left, how many stay to hold the yard (\"2 stay to hold the yard.\"); it informs and never stops **Go**.
- **Buildings (N).** **Every building has its own home, a place where it belongs, and nobody picks a plot** (round 15; the homes are in `assets/data/plots.json`, eleven in the compound since round 16, each with the name of the place and a sentence of why): the **salvage bench** by the junk pile in the south-west corner, the **greenhouse beds** against the garden's lower edge, the **workshop** under the workbench's tarp, the two **bunk houses** (numbered 1 of 2 and 2 of 2, built in that order) against the school's front wall, the two **tents** (round 16: one olive canvas and one sand, canvas ridge tents either side of the fire barrel south of the basketball court; a third, brown, is drawn for a third home), the **floodlights** and the two **watchtowers** (one either side of the gate, each reaching two night posts) on the north wall, the **radio mast** at the school's east end by the radio desk. No home is on the basketball court, the garden, the hopscotch or the four-square, the motor pool's apron, the ground in front of a school door or a ladder, the workbench's set or a prop (`tests/test_build_homes.gd` lists everything a home must avoid against the map's own rectangles). The yard is not covered in signs: a home's hammer sign and dashed outline show only while the Build screen is open, for the one home the mouse is within about three tiles of, and for a home with a site on it (with the screen shut and the mouse far from every home there is none to be seen; `--build-plots` keeps them all lit for a screenshot). Press **N** (or the hammer in the toolbar, or click a home's sign in the yard) to open the **Build screen** (with an avatar **N** opens the **Build page** of the radio panel: the same list, with Order, Cancel and Demolish, and the game does not pause; **Shift+N** opens the full screen, which is also where the lots, the street map and the walls below are): a short list of the buildings, each with what it costs, why it cannot be had now ("Can't: needs 30 scrap", "Can't: after the first one"), how far along it is or that it stands, and, for the one you pick, what it gives, its scrap and its builder-hours, where it goes (the home's name and why), and a small map of the yard with its pin (the chosen building's home is outlined in the yard too). **Build** pays the scrap at once and puts a **site** at the building's home; by day the **Builders** walk to the oldest site and build it (an hour of a builder's work counts for 1 plus 0.12 for each point of Building, less when they are hungry or shaken, so a good builder is worth two, and building teaches Building as mending walls does), a badly worn wall still comes first, and at night the crew holds the posts and the site waits. The page says who is building and about how long it will take at their pace, or that nobody is on the Builders job. **Cancel** gives all the scrap back if no work was done and half after; **Demolish** (two clicks) gives half of a building's scrap back and ends what it did. The game waits while the screen is open; pointing at a building's slip marks its home in the yard. A watchtower reaches from its own body (its shooters are those within four tiles of the tower, not of the middle of its lot). A save from before round 15 has what it had refunded (a site in full, a building by half): the old plots are no more. Eight buildings to begin with (their numbers are in `assets/data/buildings.json`): the **salvage bench** (opens the Salvage station: two more jobs that sort the yard's junk into scrap), **greenhouse beds** (two more gardeners and a little more food each dawn), the **workshop** (a third seat at the bench, quicker work, more ammo from each scrap), the **bunk house** (four beds for the hurt, better sleep, and room for four more people), the **tents** (round 16: two beds each, two of them, for 8 scrap and 6 builder-hours: room for two more people each and nothing else, no beds for the hurt and no better sleep; "Two beds. Cheap, and cold in winter."), **floodlights** (the guns reach further by night; a blackout costs no aim), the **watchtower** (on the wall: shooters near it reach three tiles further) and the **radio mast** (better news on the radio, fewer bad surprises). A game in which nothing is built plays as it always did; the first building that pays for itself is the salvage bench. With eleven homes for eight buildings (three of them may stand twice), what goes up first is the decision, not where; the streets round the school (below) are the room for more, and for the one building made only for them, the **scrap yard** (a little scrap every dawn).

  **The streets round the school: lots, mouths and walls (a small start only).** The map is a block, the compound in a ring of streets that is closed by rows of buildings at the map's edge and open at **ten places where the dead come in**. In the world an open street is a dashed amber line across it with the words "open street", drawn while the Build screen is open or the mouse is within about five tiles of it. The first time a lot could be claimed a tip says so (anchored on the toolbar's Build button), and the classic start has none of it.
- **The gate (round 22, avatar mode).** The Main gate in the north wall is the one you open and shut yourself. A **steel lever** with a **lamp** stands by it, on the apron at the west side of the opening (the lamp is green while the gate is open, red while it is shut, amber and blinking while the leaves are moving); walk up to it and **E** says **Open the gate** or **Shut the gate**, or why it cannot ("Dez is in the gateway": nothing shuts on a person), and a click on the lever does the same. **It is hard to miss (round 23):** the prompt comes within 3.5 tiles of the lever *and the front of the opening* (where a shut gate stops you when you walk straight at it, anywhere along the posts), a floating **GATE (E)** marks the lever from 12 tiles while you are inside the walls, a click on the gate's picture works it from six tiles, **Y** works it in reach and the clock's **Gate** row, clicked, does the same or glides the camera to the lever. Opening the gate by hand holds it open until the bell or until somebody shuts it; in the dark the toast says the dead can walk in. **Shutting it by hand locks it** ("Locked: cars wait outside"): a car or a party that wants to come in or go out waits at the gate, with a line in the News, until you open it again (an automatic gate, which you have not touched, still opens for them and shuts after). **Leaving the base:** walking out through the open gateway and on into the street, past about five and a half tiles from the wall, offers **the Map**: a card, **Leave the base? Past the line there is no wall: the street is the dead's, the camera, the HUD and a save go on as ever, and the edge of the map is a wall of its own. The leaves follow the real state of the gate, at any speed of the game (it takes two seconds of game time); a car or a party that has to pass asks it open by itself and it shuts again a few seconds after the last one is through. Near the lever the gateway and the lever are outlined faintly; in the last hour before the bell a gate you have held open is outlined in gold with "held open"; **the bell shuts the gate by itself** (so does the call to arms: the person at the gate post is its keeper, and a banner says it is shut), even one you held open, and **in the dark an open gate is a way in**: its ring pulses red in the yard and when the dead are near half an hour after the bell the radio raises a **Gate** call ("The gate is open and it is dark": shut it, or keep it open). The clock has a **Gate** line (a lamp and a word above the forecast: "Gate: open", "Gate: shut", "Gate: held open"; amber while it is open in the dark; a tooltip says what it is). The motor and the latch are the gate's own sounds, once for each time it moves. A tip comes once, the last hour before the first dusk: *Shut the gate at dusk: E at the gate lever*. With no avatar (the classic game, the tests, Orientation Day) the gate is as it was: it only slides open for a car or a party that is passing. - **Two levels (round 24, avatar mode).** The ground and the decks (the north catwalk, the two side walkways, the corner towers) are crossed at a ladder only: the sides of the towers are shut to the street and the ground outside, the bridge over the gateway is the deck for whoever came onto it along the catwalk and the passage for whoever came along the passage (so with the gate open the ground cannot step up onto the catwalk beside the mouth, the deck cannot step down into the passage, and a person in the passage is not on the deck and is in the gateway for the leaves, while one on the bridge, the keeper at his post, is not), a post on a deck is taken only from that deck and the gate's lever only from the ground (the prompt does not offer a post on the other level), and somebody on the ground under the bridge is drawn under its planks. The rules are `scripts/sim/levels.gd`; with no avatar nothing of it runs.
- **The school.** The dead cannot walk into the school while its two north doors hold. They go for the doors once the yard has fallen and a person is hiding inside; a door that is down opens the whole building to them. Falling back is a last stand with a clock, not a bunker.
- **The dead.** Walkers come from the first night. From night 4 some are **runners**: quick and frail, they cross the yard in a third of the time and give the crew little warning. From night 8 a **brute** comes among them: slow, huge, many shots to bring down, and it tears at a wall several times as hard as a walker. The guns shoot a brute first and a runner second. Each night is planned at dusk: most of the dead come from one or two spawn points, the later part of the night is where the brutes are.
- **The lookout.** At 17:00 (two hours before dusk, whatever the season) whoever is on the Lookout post, if they are up and about, reports what tonight looks like: about how many (give or take 15%), from which way, and whether a brute or runners are among them. Without anyone on the post nobody tells the crew.
- **Fields of fire (V).** The shooters stand at their night posts (you choose them on the Jobs screen), and a wall section is only defended if their guns reach it. The view shows each section's cover; people on the walkways and towers can be scratched far less often than people on the ground.
- **The alarm (L).** Select a wall section and set the alarm: it costs 3 scrap, rings for three game hours, and draws the dead already on their way to that section, where the guns are. One alarm at a time; not on the school doors. With an avatar and no section picked, **L** (or the toolbar's **Alarm** button) is the **call to arms** instead: everyone to their posts now, by day or before the bell, until dawn or **Stand down**.
- **People.** Each survivor has two traits (Steady, Nervous, Tinkerer, Triage, Sharp-eyed, Steady hands, Tough, Optimist, Loyal, Quick learner), bonds with some of the others (family, mentor, friend), a mood and skills that grow with use. The person panel shows all of it; the mood's tooltip lists every reason with its sign. Mood runs from Inspired through Steady and Shaken to Breaking (a shaken person works and aims a little worse); someone who breaks down stops shooting and cannot be ordered for an hour (from day 4, not on Story). A loss hurts the ones who knew them most. Shooting, Melee, Medicine and Building each gain a point with enough use, at most three over where they started. People speak up when something happens (the bell, a wound, a friend down) and the others say a few words when one is lost.
- **The radio hour.** At noon whoever is at the Radio desk (Ruth to begin with) has news on the radio: about two days in three there is a card (never on the first day or the last). Fifteen cards: heavy rain, fog, a blackout that Dez must mend, a horde warning, stragglers at the wall by day, a fever, supplies falling from the sky, a stranger at the gate, a cry for help, a trade offer, an argument... A card with a choice waits for you and states its risk plainly; if nobody answers within two game hours its default is taken. A hidden tension leans the draw: after a bad stretch the radio is kinder. The morning after each night an assembly card says how many were put down and by whom, what the wall lost and gained, who was lost (and what the others said), what was learned and how the crew is.
- **A small start, and recruits over the radio.** A new game begins with **five** survivors, Ruth, Dez, Marcus, Ana and Walt (**Crew of five** on the New game screen, the choice it opens on; **Small crew (3)** begins with Ruth, Dez and Marcus and is the hard start). The others call in over the radio. From day 2, at the noon radio hour, if the compound has a bed free and someone is at the Radio desk (the first callers, Ana, Walt and Hector, come through even with the desk empty; a stranger needs someone there), a caller can come through: the card **A voice on the radio** asks you to **tell them the way in** (they reach the gate at 07:00 next morning and join the crew, with a bell, a banner and a new card in the crew bar) or say **Not yet** (they call again in two days). The callers who are not in the crew are, in order, Ana, Walt and Hector; after them the callers are strangers (a name, a trade, skills, two traits, a weapon), eight at the most in a run. What caps the crew is **beds**: the school has six, each **bunk house** adds four and each **tent** two (the Build screen's foot says how many the compound has beds for), so the crew can grow past six: to ten with the two tents, fourteen with the two bunk houses, eighteen with all four; with none free no call comes, and a call that was already on the table cannot be answered with the way in ("No bed for them"). A call comes every three days at the most (two with a **radio mast**). A newcomer may be **bitten**: they arrive bleeding and the infection rules take over, so the gate has a decision in it (treat them with **T**, or send them out with **X**). In a small start the nights follow the size of the crew: three people face about 60% of the night that six face, and a bigger crew a bigger night (at most 1.6 times). The crew bar fits a crew of fourteen (the cards shrink and then wrap into rows). A save keeps who has come, who is on the way and who is still to call. (There used to be a third start, all six from day one with no recruits, no town and no avatar; it is gone, and an old save of it loads as an avatar game: see the Controls.)
- **Orientation Day.** A guided first run (about eight minutes, a Story game that cannot be lost) taught in the game the player will then play, with Ruth as the avatar and the town on (so G opens the Town Board): **eight short steps** (round 26: cut down from thirteen for the demo; the News and its call, the map, the alarm and the duty roster are no steps now, and the last card says in a line where to find them). *This is you* (the YOU tag), *Walk* (W A S D), *Aim and shoot* (a lone walker comes to the north wall: shots stop at the wall's face, so climb the marked ladder to the walkway, hold the right mouse button to aim, then click to shoot), *Reload and put away* (R reloads; Q puts the weapon away and draws it again), *Ask, do not order* (click Dez's picture and press Come with me; Ana is bandaging Walt, so she refuses and the reason shows; Insist pushes her at a cost of trust), *The radio wheel* (hold C, move onto Hold here, let go), *The gate and the bell* (it is late afternoon and the gate stands open: walk to the gate lever and press E to shut it before the 19:00 bell; Speed up hurries the day, the bell shuts a gate that was left open, and the dead of the dusk come as it rings) and the last card (the briefing card each morning, T for the News, G for the map and the pickup trip, nights at the wall). **Skip tutorial** is on every card (a small link at the top right) and in the Esc menu while the lesson is on: it goes to the last card, "Skipped", whose Keep playing here lets the game go on as a normal one (the lesson's lock lifted, the nights real), Start a real run begins a new game and Replay begins the lesson again. Each step has a condition the rules can check (`scripts/tutorial_conditions.gd`) and none names a key the avatar does not have. The lessons are `assets/data/tutorial.json`. (`assets/data/tutorial_classic.json`, the lesson of the old yard with no avatar, is only a fixture for tests of the coach: no player can reach it.) Offered on the first launch (a card that says what it teaches); replay it from the title screen. After that a few one-line tips show once each in real runs (first dusk, low stores, the first wound, the first runner and brute, the day the crew can first afford to build, and the Town Board in a small start); switch them off in Settings.
- **Menus and settings.** The title screen offers Continue (the newest save), New game (difficulty, crew, length and seed), Orientation Day, Load, Settings and Credits; **Esc** with nothing selected opens the pause menu, and so does the menu button next to the sound button. - **The goal.** Hold thirty nights. Night 30 is the Big Night: more than twice the usual number of the dead, a third of them runners and brutes, coming from everywhere (with an avatar, days 10 and 20 are Big Nights too: see Nights). At the dawn that starts day 31 a convoy is on the road and the run is won.

## The avatar game

A new game has an **avatar**: one survivor you play (Ruth), with the rest of the crew as a community that answers, refuses and sometimes proposes. Every rule below is behind avatar mode (`sim.player_id != ""`), so a game with none (the balance harness and the tests: the simulation's own no-player mode, which no player can start) plays exactly as the old yard did. The rules are `scripts/sim/{avatar,radio,interact,nights,night_calls,night_data,relations,specialists,initiative}.gd`, the road is `scripts/drive/`, the pages are in `scripts/ui/`; the design is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-03-avatar-and-radio-design.md` and each round has a plan in `docs/superpowers/plans/`.

- **Asking, not ordering (the radio).** Click a crew card and ask: **Come with me** (they follow), **Hold here**, **Cover me** (they stand behind your aim), **Search that** (the container under the pointer, on a trip), **Go back**, **To the walls**. The answer comes after a delay and in a tone that follow the person's **trust** in you (0 to 100, 55 to begin with: half a second from a trusted friend, up to four from a doubtful one). A refusal is never random and always says why, where you asked: they are down, bleeding, starving or shaken, or busy (nursing a patient, at the radio desk at noon, on the lookout at the forecast), a friend of theirs lies hurt, or an enemy of theirs is in the squad. A soft refusal can be overridden with **Insist** (trust -6, mood -4); a hard one cannot. Trust rises with a word a day (+1) and with a person coming back safe from a trip you led, and falls when you insist or someone hurt was following your request.
- **Calls.** A call is a card with two to four options and a countdown after which its default is taken; it never pauses the game. The most urgent shows above the gun, the rest are counted in the News (T). The calls: a wall under attack, a wall that is going, a plated brute coming, a breach, the night's forecast, a fever, a stranger at the gate, the road home at dusk, an idea. When the survivor you play falls, the next in line takes over (everyone's trust in the new one is ten lower and the base mourns).
- **Home: the radio panel.** **T** opens one non-modal panel at the right, with four pages. **Jobs**: a row for each person with their day job and their night post; picking one *asks*, and they answer in their own words or refuse with the reason in amber (with Insist for a soft refusal); **Fill empty seats** and the stations' settings are there too. **Build**: the buildings with their cost or why one cannot be ordered, **Order**, **Cancel** and **Demolish**, and a note when nobody is on the Builders job. **Map**: the Town Board's places, the people and the vehicles as chips, **Take control** and **Ask names to go**, the Big Night warning for a trip, Claim and Recall. **News**: the log and the calls that wait. The game does not pause, WASD still walks, and the camera keeps the avatar clear of the panel. The old modal pages stay behind **Shift+J / N / G** and each page's **Full page** button; the lots, the street map and the walls are there only. The Map page exists in a settlement game only (`scripts/ui/{radio_panel,jobs_page,build_page,map_page,map_data,news_panel}.gd`).
- **E at the base.** The pill beside the nearest thing in reach says what E does. A **person**: a talk card with what they are doing in their own words, what they need (bleeding, fever, hungry, shaken, no job, out of ammo, each with a button to the page that deals with it), their trust in you and the verbs; the first word of a day is worth +1 trust. A **post**: you take it and the holder takes yours (refused while the post is manned and its holder stands at it); standing still on it narrows your spread by 30%. The **workbench**: twelve seconds of making ammo from scrap at one and a half times the usual rate; it ends when you move or fire. A damaged **wall** (a compound section under 95% or down): you mend it from the scrap, one press goes on until the wall is whole, the scrap is gone, you move or fire, you are hurt or one of the dead is on you; allowed under fire. A parked **car**: its fuel and how far it goes.
- **Errands.** **Ask names to go** on the Map page sends people on a trip without you, after the radio's delay, as a dice trip with a penalty: about 60% of the loot, no rare finds, no rescue, no vehicle brought home, a worse chance of clearing a place, wounds one and a half times as likely and losses far likelier. The page writes the terms under the button and each person's refusal; the News says when they set off and what came back. A trip you lead has none of the penalty.
- **Nights.** The nights are sized so that the community holds an ordinary night alone when its posts are manned and its walls decent (the clock's forecast line says "Should hold", or what is missing). Standing among the defenders makes them shoot better: within 9 tiles of you (the faint ring on the ground) their aim is 15% truer and they fire 18% faster. A **Big Night** needs you: day 10 (the **Siege**: plated brutes at one wall) and day 20 (the **Rush**: a wave of runners, brutes behind it), the same on days 10 and 20 of each Campaign season, and the last night of every season. Most of its dead come to one wall; its brutes are **plated**: a shot at the body does a tenth, a hit on the head (the ring over it) three times, and a defender finds the head one shot in thirty, so the guns pile on a brute while the wall goes and you shoot it in the head. A **breach** (a wall that has fallen) is shut under fire only by you with E, or by two defenders you stand guard for. The calls of the night: *the wall is going* (hold, send two, I'm on my way, fall back), *a plated brute is coming* (keep the guns on it, I'll take it, send two, draw it to another wall with the lure for 3 scrap), *the wall is down* (hold, close it, pull back, I'm on my way) and *tonight* (understood, call to arms, an alarm on a wall); say "on my way" and be there within fifteen minutes or lose trust. **The forecast is a tool:** at noon of the day before a Big Night, with someone at the radio desk, the News and a badge by the day counter say "Big Night tomorrow" with the side, the walls and the hour to be home; at 17:00 the lookout repeats it with the walls' and posts' readiness, and the target walls wear a hatched bracket. On a trip at dusk the place's panel shows "Dusk in 2:10, road home 1:20, leave by 15:40" and a call lets you head home or stay out (the base then holds alone). The numbers are in `assets/data/nights.json`; `tools/avatar_bot.gd` plays the avatar for the harness (STATUS.md has the tables).
- **A community with ideas.** People who work, eat and stand watch together become **friends**, some fray into **rivals**, a **couple** may form, and the News says so ("Dez and Ana are close now"); the person panel lists who they are close to and at odds with. A friend in the squad warms a request, someone whose friend lies hurt stays with them and says so, an enemy of someone in the squad will not go, and a push on Dez costs a little trust with his friends. One person becomes **the voice**, the one people turn to, and their opinion shows under a call ("Ana, the medic: treat them."). People earn **titles** by skill and practice (the medic, the engineer, the marksman, the scout) with a small bonus on that work and first call for the seat; when the holder falls the camp has no medic, mourns it, and the bonus is gone until an heir is named. From day 3, by day and never at the radio hour, someone brings an **idea** over the radio in their own words with its price ("I could fix the east wall properly. About 20 scrap."), at most two waiting and about one a day: mend a wall, build something, raise the bench's ammo line or the wall crew's, sit at an empty desk, lend a hand in the garden, go on an errand, take a better post, a post beside a friend, a shared supper, settle a quarrel, a lesson, a farewell for the dead. **Yes** does it for real through the game's own actions, **later** changes nothing, **no** costs the proposer a little trust and mood, and three noes in five days make them quiet for three days. Small start only; Orientation Day has none (`scripts/sim/{relations,specialists,initiative}.gd`, `assets/data/{chemistry,titles,ideas}.json`).
- **The live drive.** A trip you lead is a drive (or a walk) down a road to the place, then the place, then the same road home, with no loading card between. Each leg takes **30 to 90 real seconds** whatever the road (a long road raises the pace the base runs at, not the time), and the base runs behind you with its calls on screen. **The clock is the bonus:** the minutes a leg takes are the base's own, so driving fast and clean arrives early (up to a third of the road's minutes saved), and crashes, grass and stops cost minutes. The car (sedan, pickup, van or bus) has weight; you aim and fire from the window, the squad shoots from theirs, a dead one at speed is run over (a brute costs speed and hit points), wrecks cost hit points, and a car at 0 hit points ends the drive and the rest is on foot. Fuel is the abstract trip's: the gauge shows the trip tank draining by distance. One to four events each leg, never the same twice: an ambush, a roadblock (thread the gap, ram a wreck, or **Clear it** on foot), a survivor (take them in if there is a bed and a seat), a wreck with supplies, a puncture, a herd, a fuel stash, a checkpoint (pay, trade, talk or run it) and a smoke sign; and a **night road** with headlights and more of the dead when the way home crosses dusk. A stop opens a card (**1 2 3** or a click; a refused option says why; it takes its default after 8 s, and the base is not paused). A trip on foot is the same road at walking pace with the same events (no car, so no breakdown, ram or fuel). A party that gets home at night comes in. Road, events and cars: `scripts/drive/`, `assets/data/road_events.json`, `road_layouts.json`; the pace and the hand-off: `scripts/raid/place_flow.gd`.

- **A car to begin with (round 24).** A new game of the avatar kind (the real game's New game, a small start of three or five) begins with **one pickup** parked in the first bay of the motor pool by the Main gate, in good repair (its whole hp, 90) and fuelled with **10 fuel**: two trips to the farthest of the five places the Map shows on the first morning (the pickup burns 3.26 fuel to the houses and 4.95 to the church and back, so ten covers two of any of them), and a News line says so ("The pickup is fuelled: Take control on the Map (G) to drive it"). The Map's **Take control** and the live drive use it from day 1 like any vehicle of the fleet (a trip burns its fuel at the gate; fuel found on the road tops the tank up). Only that run has it: the old yard, the harness, a test's game, Orientation Day and a **loaded save** (an older save has no starting car and is not given one; a new game's save keeps its car) do not. The rules are `scripts/sim/vehicles.gd` (`give_starting_car`, `starting_fuel`), called by `main.gd` for a new run of the real game (`{"car": true}` in a test's run); the test is `tests/test_r24b_life.gd`.

*Round 22 (easier controls and a real gate) is in progress on branch `feature/r22-controls-gate`; this README does not describe it yet.*

## Base life

Round 24, "something is happening in the base". The crew is three to five people at their jobs, so the yard also carries a few small things that move by themselves, all decoration (nothing is solid, nothing is saved, no rule reads them, the no-avatar promise is untouched). The manager is `scripts/render/base_life.gd`, made by `main.gd` next to the gate and the parked cars; each part is a small node of its own in `scripts/render/` with a header that says how it works:

- **Flags** (`flag_node.gd`): a teal-over-cream flag on each lookout tower, waving in the wind, harder in rain.
- **The radio mast** (`mast_node.gd`): on the school's brick corner over the radio desk, a red lamp that blinks slowly (with a red halo in the glow layer at night), a dish that turns and two aerials that sway.
- **The school bell** (`bell_node.gd`): the title's brass bell on a wooden frame by the gate, hanging still in the wind and **swinging, with pale rings going out from it, when the rules ring a bell**: the dusk bell in full (with the bell sound the game already plays), the call to arms' bell smaller, a season turning wide and slow, a building finished a tap.
- **The feel of a fight** (`scripts/render/combat_fx.gd`, polish wave P-COMBAT; render and audio only: the rules tell it what happened through `Sim.feel`, which is null in the tests and the harness, and it has a random source and a clock of its own, so a fight plays the same with it and without it). A muzzle flash by weapon (a pale star and a glow, a fan of light for the pump gun) with a short warm light, the gun's smoke, a casing that pops out and rings on the ground (a few at a time); the survivor's body kicks back and the camera is pushed (more for the pump gun); a hit throws blood and a puff of it and flashes white on the struck one, which is knocked back; **plates** ring with blue-white sparks and a flash of the steel instead of bleeding; a bullet in a wall throws sparks, chips and dust; the dead fall, slide away from the blow and raise dust where they land, and a kill within three tiles holds the picture for 50 ms (the rules' clock does not stop); the splats they leave fade in about twenty seconds (150 at most, pooled). A blow of the survivor's has a bright trail and a sparkle where it lands. An empty gun clicks, flashes the word at the survivor and flashes the ammunition readout; the pump gun's reload ring shows a pip and a click for each shell; the readout flashes when a low magazine is fired. **Reduce motion** turns off the push, the hit-stop, the sprite's nudge, the white flash and the sweep of the trail; the muzzle's light is half as bright. `tools/combat_bench.gd` measures what a fight costs to draw.
- **Washing on a line** (`laundry_node.gd`): five pieces in the quiet south-east of the yard, swinging in the wind (harder in rain).
- **A stray dog** (`dog_node.gd`): trots about the yard by day along a small graph of the walking grid's open ground, sniffs, lies down by a fire; at dusk it goes to the nearest fire and sleeps (it breathes). A two-frame walk, a wagging tail.
- **Pigeons** (`birds_node.gd`): perch on the school's wall, the walkway's rail and the towers' sandbags; **a shot, the groan of one of the dead close by, the bell or a person coming too near flushes them**, they fly off the screen and come back after some seconds of quiet; they roost at dusk and return with the day.
- **Leaves and litter** (`litter_node.gd`): autumn leaves and scraps of paper blown across the yard, tumbling, faster in a gust; not in rain.
- **Puddle ripples** (`ripples_node.gd`): pale rings opening on the ground while the rules' weather is rain.
- **Two friends chatting** (`chat_node.gd`): friends (the old yard's bonds, or the avatar game's friends, couples and family) who stand together at rest get a bubble of three dots that passes between them.
- **Lanterns that gutter** (`lighting.gd`, `lamp_flicker`): the oil lanterns flicker a lot as they come up at dusk and a little all night.

The wind is a slow base with gusts (`wind`, 0.05 to 1.4: stronger in rain, a little less in fog); everything runs on the game's pace (paused: still; 3x: faster, like the flames). **Cost:** the flags, the washing, the leaves and the ripples are one draw call each and move on the GPU (a vertex shader, or a MultiMesh moved by a shader, from two numbers set each frame); the mast, the bell, the dog, the birds and the chat bubbles are baked meshes moved by a shader too, one draw call each, and only while on the screen; a part off the screen is hidden. Measured in a real window (`tools/life_bench.gd`: the life switched on and off in blocks of frames under the same smoke), it adds **about 4 draw calls** with the whole yard in view (627 against 623), 5 near the gate at the usual zoom (577 against 571) and 4 on a rainy night (278 against 274), and no measurable script time (one `update` of the life is about 0.02 ms; the renderer's CPU time differs by under 0.03 ms). **Reduce motion** (Settings) hangs the flags and the washing, stops the dish and the bell, keeps the lamp lit, hides the leaves, the ripples and the birds and has the dog lie by its fire. **`--calm`** leaves all of it out (a screenshot helper); the title's backdrop has none of it. Not done from the wish list: porters carrying crates, a cook at a pot, someone sweeping or hanging washing, a sentry pacing and a mechanic under a hood (they need a person to do them, and the crew's own people already have their jobs: `scripts/render/work_anim.gd`), tents and sleepers, and a chimney (the fire barrels already smoke: `fx.smoke`).

## Rain in the town

A town run has weather (RAIN, the first thing a tester asked for: "we need to add rain effects sometimes"). About **one day in two** has ONE rain period of **2 to 5 game hours** (a game hour is half a real minute, so one to two and a half real minutes), **a drizzle (40 percent), a steady rain (35) or a storm (25)** (LOOK2: it was 50, 35, 15, and nobody saw a lightning), beginning at a seeded hour between 07:00 and 23:00 (so some run on past midnight). The rain eases in over a third of a game hour and out over 0.4; the sky is OVERCAST for 0.8 of an hour before and after. **The first two game hours of a run are dry** (nothing falls in its first ten minutes); nothing else is held back: **it may rain on the first attack night, a storm included**, and the lab shows the balance holds: `tools/house_lab.gd --seeds=1..16 --mode=bot,idle --weather-first-night=storm` held the first attack night in **16 of 16 runs with the bot and 5 of 16 idle**, exactly as with `--weather-first-night=clear` (16 and 5): of the 32 runs one differs, by under two points of wall health (the rain covers a few shots, but the dead of the first night are the wave's, not the street's, and the street is held asleep through it). The school (`--school`, the demo, the no-player sim) has none of this: its own weather, `supplies.gd` `roll_dawn`, is as it was.

- **The rules' side** (`scripts/town/skies.gd`, `sim.skies`, a TownSim part). The schedule is a PURE function of the run's seed and the game clock: nothing is rolled as the run goes (a `RandomNumberGenerator` of its own is seeded from the seed and the day, and no die of the rules' is thrown for it: the golden files are untouched), so the same seed rains at the same hours every time, and a save needs nothing of its own. `sim.weather` says `clear`, `rain` (drizzle or steady) or `storm` (the words the school's systems already read); the clock's word is `clear`, `overcast`, `drizzle`, `rain` or `storm` and the icon beside the temperature shows it (a cloud, drops, a bolt: `scripts/ui/weather_glyph.gd`; another on the minimap's rim; each has a tooltip). The dawn's barrels give their rain water when it rained in the night just ended. **Effects on play, light:** the dead's noise carries **a fifth less far** while it rains (`field.noise_mult` 0.8: a shot, a shout), the light is poorer (below), and in a **storm the crew's scavengers stay in** ("It is pouring: not now"; a radio order to search is refused with "it is pouring"; a run already out goes on).
- **What you see** (`scripts/render/rain_view.gd`, a view part; one draw call for the rain, one for the splashes): ONE full-screen quad on a layer over the world and under the HUD (`rain.gdshader`): three depth layers of streaks worked out from a hash of the pixel (no texture is read), slanting with the wind (steeper and gustier in a storm), thicker with the rain, dim at night and brighter in the pool round the avatar with a faint mist there. **Indoors** (the avatar in a house, her roof cut away) it fades out over half a second and the rain bed on the Rain bus (`default_bus_layout.tres`, sent to Ambience) closes a low-pass (780 Hz, 4.5 dB down): the roof, not the street. **Splashes**: up to 30 rings opening on the ground round the avatar (a MultiMesh, `rain_splash.gdshader`), outdoors only, a few in a drizzle and many in a storm. **Wet ground**: the outdoor ground meshes (lawn, road, sidewalk, dirt, gravel: about a dozen draws that were there already, not a shader for each tile) wear `wet_ground.gdshader` while the ground is wet (it soaks in nine seconds and dries in a minute): darker and cooler, puddles on the road, and a soft glint that slides over wet paving (and shows near a lamp at night, as the light multiplies it); a dry day has no material at all. **The light**: `lighting.set_extra_dim(v)` with 0.15 in a drizzle, 0.3 in steady rain and 0.45 in a storm (a little under cloud), eased, on top of the day's grade (the night takes less of it). **Lightning** (LOOK2): **in a storm every 11 to 32 seconds, in a STEADY rain every 30 to 75 (weaker, and a farther, softer thunder 2.5 to 4.5 s later), none in a drizzle.** The schedule is the rules' (`skies.strikes_between`, a pure function of the run's seed and the game clock, its own hash, no die of the rules': a reloaded save has the same sky; it runs with the clock, so at x3 the strikes come three times as often in real seconds). A strike is a **VISIBLE FORKED BOLT** (one mesh, one draw call: a bright jagged stroke from the top of the view to a ground point in it, never on the avatar, with one or two short branches and a halo where it lands, alive 0.12 s and fading), **the world lit in two pulses** (a bright one, a dip at 0.1 s, a weaker one, a fade over 0.7 s: the dark look's grade lifts the scene's own colours by a cold white-blue, `lighting.grade.set_flash`, so that the shapes of the night and the dead show, and a veil adds the glare; with `--nodarklook` the veil alone) and **thunder** (in a storm 1 to 3 s later by distance as before, one of the SOUND pass's three files; **about one storm bolt in eight is NEAR**: a heavy flash, a thick bolt with two branches and the near crack within 0.3 s). Between the bolts a dim **distant sheet flash** now and then (no bolt, no sound). **Reduce flashes**: no bolt and no lift, only the slow gentle brightening there always was; **Reduce motion**: about half the strikes and no sheet flashes. Cosmetic only: no damage, no rule changes. For the rain itself **Reduce motion** gives about half the streaks, no near layer, and a slower fall. The rain stops with the game (paused: it hangs).
- **What you hear** (`scripts/audio/soundscape.gd`, the rain bed): it follows the weather word: a murmur in a drizzle, full in steady rain, loud in a storm (`skies.sound_level()`), fades in and out over a few seconds, and is muffled indoors (above: under a roof the Rain bus's low-pass closes to 1300 Hz and the bed loses about 10 LU). **Thunder** (the SOUND pass: `thunder_near/mid/far_*.wav`, `tools/make_weather_sounds.py`) follows the flash by 1 to 3 s as before and is one of three by how far the strike is, on the Rain bus too; the levels of the rain, the thunder and everything else are the MIX TABLE in `docs/audio-mix.md`.
- **Staging.** `--weather=drizzle|rain|storm|clear` holds the town's sky for the run (`--weather=fog` and the school's words are the school's); `--weather-first-night=storm|rain|drizzle|clear` holds it only through the first attack night (with `--stage=night` for a shot of it); `--lightning` holds a flash at its brightest for the shot (no thunder); `--norain` leaves the whole part out; `--rain-cost` prints its script time and the frame's draw calls at frame 120 (give `--frames=130`). The lab takes `--weather-first-night=` too (`tools/house_lab.gd`).
- **Cost** (an M1, a real window; the machine was busy, so the milliseconds are upper bounds). **Draw calls** with `--rain-cost` at frame 130 in the same scene: a clear sky 344, a storm 347 at 1280x800; 342 and 345 at 2560x1440: **3 more** (the rain quad, the splashes' MultiMesh, the two icons, which are 24-pixel pictures made in code once, a few milliseconds each, one at a time from half a second into the run: `weather_glyph.gd`); the wet ground adds none (the meshes it is put on were drawn already). **Script time** of the part: about 0.07 ms a frame clear and 0.1 ms in a storm with 30 splashes. **The shaders** read no texture at all (the brief allowed about twelve reads): the rain is 3 layers of a hash, the wet ground 3 value noises. **GPU time**, measured alone (the display runs at 120 Hz, so one quad is under the cap: 24 full-screen copies were stacked and the difference divided): a rain quad at 2560x1440 is **0.41 ms in a drizzle, 0.49 steady, 0.56 in a storm, 0.60 in a storm at night** (it grows with the pixels: about a third of that at 1280x800), and the wet-ground shader 0.63 ms for a screen of nothing but wet ground (a view is about half of it, and the lawn skips the glint). The web build is three to four times slower: about 0.5 ms at 1280x800 and 2 ms at 2560x1440 for the rain; `Reduce motion` drops the near layer and half the streaks. In the game itself, with the rain switched on and off in blocks of twelve frames, the frame time did not move (8.36 against 8.47 ms in a storm at 2560x1440, held at the 120 Hz display's 8.33; 10.03 against 10.15 ms in steady rain at 1280x800).

## The dead of the town (DEAD1)

**More of the dead (2026-10-10, PH10, the owner's "more zombies").** The street has about **60 percent more** of the dead by day (a 192 x 192 town: about 410 in the streets before, about 650 now; 700 before in all, about 950 now), and the night's cap of the dead alive at once went from 40 to **70**. Every number is data. `assets/data/town_dead.json` is the generator's (`TownGen.dead_data()`): `total` (700 for a whole town), `yard_share`, and `street` (`group`, `still`, `runner`, `brute`, **`extra`**: the street's ambient dead as a share of the street's first ones, **`ramp`**: [[tiles from the nearest calm point (the entry, every candidate home), the share of the tried places that is kept], ...]: none inside 25 tiles, 0.4 at 40, all of it from 70, so the first day's walk, search and rescues are what the lab tuned them for and the town beyond is thick with the dead). The new ones come from a stream of their own after the old ones, so every other part of every seed's town is the same, and `TownGen.VERSION` is **4** (older saves are refused with the card: docs/save-compat.md). `assets/data/one_house.json`: `big_night.cap` 70 (the first attack night), `night.cap_dusk` 50 and `night.cap_night` 70 (every other night: waves.gd still has the colony's 30 and 50, the town asks `OneHouse.night_cap`). The size of a night stays a share: `night.first` and `night.growth` (a cap is only how many may be alive at once, not how many come).

**What 70 at once cost, and what was done** (`tools/dead_probe.gd`, headless; `tools/town_bench.gd --what=game --scenes=night --wave=70 --near=1`, windowed; the numbers are in STATUS.md section 0). With the fence up the dead that find no free place at it muster in the street and ask again every second, and the search for a place tried the barred doors first: their approach points lie inside the yard, there is no way to them from the street, and a search that finds none reads the whole of the town (4 ms each; 40 of the dead asking was a step of 14 to 40 ms). `TownRules._walled_off` now knows it after the first failed search (the closed pocket round the place is kept until a tile of the walking grid is shut or opened, `Pathfinder.rev`), `dead_inside` asks the yard once and not for each of the dead, and a slow machine (the adaptive cap of `TownPerf`) holds the night's dead to the same share it halves the street's awake dead by (`TownRules.wave_cap`: 70 becomes 35, never under 24). Tools: `tools/dead_census.gd --seeds=1..8 [--dense]` (where the generator puts the dead: houses, yards, streets, by distance from the entry; `--dense` the busiest places for a picture), `tools/dead_probe.gd --what=night|walk` (the cost of a rules step by how many of the dead are awake), `tools/town_bench.gd` `--wave=N --near=1`.

## Seasons and the campaign

A new game has two lengths, chosen on the New game screen under **Length** (the choice is remembered, like the crew's): **Autumn: thirty nights**, the game as it has always been, and **Campaign: the four seasons**, Autumn, Winter, Spring and Summer in turn (102 days), each with its own rules and its own Big Night. Either start (small crew or full) plays either length.

- **The Big Night is the last night of every season** (night 30, then 54, 78 and 102; with an avatar the 10th and 20th day of every season are Big Nights too, see Nights), and the season turns at the dawn after it: the banner says "Winter begins" and gives the season's line, a deep bell rings, and a one-line tip about the season comes once (not in a game that is not a campaign). Surviving the last season's Big Night wins ("The seasons are over"); a defeat says which season it came in ("You fell in Winter, on day 41"). A season's nights are sized as if the game were older: each season gone makes the nights six days older, and the season's own factor for the nights is on top.
- **What each season changes** (the numbers are in `assets/data/seasons.json`; Autumn repeats the old constants, and a game that is not a campaign is Autumn for ever). The hours are the real clock's: the bell rings at dusk, the night runs from an hour and a half after it until an hour before the day. The beds' yield, what a person eats and drinks, the road's hours on the Town Board and the size of the nights are multipliers of the Autumn game, and the Big Night's factor is over its day's budget:

| Season | Days | Dusk | Day | Beds (garden) | Food / water used | Roads (travel hours) | Nights | Big Night | Weather and cold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Autumn | 30 | 19:00 | 6:30 | x1 | x1 / x1 | x1 | x1 | x2.4 | 33% rain (0% of it snow), 7 to 17 C |
| Winter | 24 | 16:30 | 7:30 | x0.15 | x1.15 / x0.9 | x1.3 | x1.15 | x2.6 | 40% rain (70% of it snow), -5 to 3 C |
| Spring | 24 | 19:30 | 6:00 | x0.8 | x1 / x0.9 | x1 | x1.2 | x2.8 | 55% rain (0% of it snow), 4 to 14 C |
| Summer | 24 | 21:00 | 5:30 | x1.3 | x1 / x1.3 | x0.9 | x1.1 | x3 | 12% rain (0% of it snow), 18 to 30 C |

- **What you see.** The clock says the season and the day in it ("WINTER, DAY 5 OF 24"; the short form "WINTER 5/24" in a narrow window and while paused), counts to the season's dusk and day, draws its strip with them, and gives the season's temperature and the weather ("-4C, snow"). The light is mapped to the season's day (Winter's dusk falls at 16:30, Summer's at 21:00) and tinted: Winter cold and blue, Spring a little green, Summer warm; Autumn is as it was. On a snow day (Winter only) soft flakes drift down and sideways, slowly, and the ground wears a light white cover that is lighter on the other Winter days. All of it is drawn in code, no art; under reduced motion the flakes fall at a third of the pace and do not sway.
- **Saves** keep the season and the day it began on; a save from before the campaign loads as Autumn.

## How it fits together

- `scripts/main.gd`: builds everything at startup, then runs the camera,
  input, the event→sound/HUD wiring and the frame loop.
- `scripts/world.gd`: the static map. The ground is a `TileMapLayer`; props,
  vehicles, wall pieces and roofs are sprites in draw order. It also owns the
  walkability grids.
- `scripts/sim/`: the simulation. `sim.gd` holds the clock, people, walkers,
  combat and wall damage; its modules are `waves.gd` (tonight's plan and the dead
  who come), `kinds.gd` (walker, runner, brute), `tactics.gd` (the alarm, the Builders' repairs,
  coverage and the lookout's forecast), supplies, medical, scavenge and scenario.
  `pathfinder.gd` wraps `AStarGrid2D` and smooths paths.
  `step_clock.gd` hands out whole 1/30 s steps, so the rules run at 30 Hz at any
  frame rate and a seed plays the same game everywhere; the drawing interpolates.
- `scripts/render/`: drawing.
  - `pawn_node.gd` draws one person, walker, runner or brute (y-sorted).
  - `pawn_art.gd` has the live legs, arms and hands, and the build of each kind of the dead (size, lean, tint).
  - `fire_node.gd` draws flames.
  - `fx.gd` handles effects.
  - `lighting.gd` covers the day/night sky (`CanvasModulate`) and every lamp (`PointLight2D`); in a campaign it maps the hour to the season's day and tints the sky with the season's tint.
  - `snow.gd` draws the snow: flakes on the screen on a snow day and the white cover over the outdoor ground.
  - `glow_node.gd` draws additive glows (and, round 24, whatever is put in its `extras`: the radio mast's red halo).
  - `base_life.gd` (round 24) makes and runs the base's little life (flags, the radio mast, the school bell, washing, a dog, birds, leaves, ripples, chatting friends: see Base life above), with `life_kit.gd` (a mesh builder and the cloth shaders).
  - `overlay_node.gd` draws the names, bars and brackets, the fields-of-fire view and the alarm's marker.
- `scripts/ui/hud.gd`: the HUD, built from Controls; `scavenge_dialog.gd` and `game_over.gd` are its modals (in a small start the Town Board, `town_board.gd` with the paper map of `town_map.gd`, takes the dialog's place). replacement PNG at the same size drops straight in.

- `assets/objects`, `walls`, `terrain`: props, the school walls, ground textures.
- `scripts/render/deck_faces.gd`: draws what makes the walkways and towers look raised: the scaffold face under the planks, the tower bases, the supports along the side walkways, the shadows and the ladders (their sizes are constants at the top of the file). The pieces it draws are `assets/objects/deck_*.png`, drawn in code by `tools/make_deck_art.py` (Python with Pillow and NumPy: flat colours with a dark outline). The walkways can only be climbed at the ladders; `world.gd` shuts the tiles under the face for people and for the dead.
- `assets/pawns`, `portraits`, `weapons`, `trees`, `fx`: characters and effects.
- `assets/ui/icons`: HUD icons. `assets/ui/theme`: the noticeboard frames, built by `tools/make_ui_theme.py`, which draws its materials (slate, chalk dust, oak, brass, paper, card) from seeded noise. The game has no AI-made pictures (`docs/assets-log.md`).
- `assets/audio`: sound effects and ambience loops; the UI sounds (`ui_*.wav`) are synthesised by `tools/make_ui_sounds.py`; the gunfight's (the shots of each gun, a bullet in flesh, on steel and in a wall, a blow and its whoosh, the body falling, an empty gun's click, the magazine, the bolt, a shell, the pump, brass on the ground) by `tools/make_combat_sounds.py` (peaks near -3 dBFS for the shots; `scripts/audio/combat_sounds.gd` declares the new names, `sound.gd` loads them).
- `assets/data/map.json`: the map layout, lights, wall segments and roster.

They were generated once from the original web prototype, which has since
been removed. Godot is the only version now.

## Demo

**Three Bells** is a demo of the game: the first three nights of one run, the same every time (a fixed seed and a crew of five), with a clear end, for a stranger to feel the core loop in 20 to 30 minutes. It is the same game with a different front: `--demo` (or the custom feature tag `demo` of an export) switches it on, and the full game is untouched without it.

```bash
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --path . -- --demo          # the title says DEMO; New game begins the three nights, no choices to make
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --path . -- --demo --shot=/tmp/end.png --endcard   # the end card (the colony plays its three days alone, quickly)
```

**What it keeps:** the survivor you play (move, aim, shoot, holster, the command wheel, right-click orders), the radio and the community (the verbs, trust, refusals, and the ideas, which begin on day 2), the nights at the wall (the lookout's forecast, the wall calls, the real gate with its lever and the dusk keeper), the base and its look, one trip (the starting pickup driven to the pharmacy, searched, driven home), and the morning briefing with its priorities (from day 2). Night 3 is a Big Night, a Siege with plated brutes at one wall; nights 1 and 2 are ordinary and gentle. The first morning's alert and News line send the pickup to the pharmacy (the stores are short of medicine).

**What it hides** (kept in the code, switched off by `scripts/sim/demo_run.gd`, the demo's scenario; every part asks `sim.is_demo()`): the four dials, the trouble in the yard and the fever dilemma (`policies`, `problems`, `dilemmas`: `enabled = false`, as Orientation Day does), who is close to whom and the specialists (`relations`, `specialists`), the seasons, the lots and the streets of the dead, and the long-term building (the Build page offers the salvage bench, workshop, bunk house, tents, floodlights and watchtower alone, and has no streets button); the Map has only the houses and the pharmacy (the rest of the town is not on the board at all); the road's events are five kinds (ambush, roadblock, survivor, wreck supplies, breakdown); the Map page has no errand options (Loot, Scout, Clear, Ask to go, Claim, Recall: the one trip is Take control); the radio panel has no Jobs tab (the Base page's "Jobs (advanced)" and Shift+J still open it) and the Base page no Policies or Trouble sections.

**The end.** After the dawn of day 4 (three nights held) the run ends on a card, "Thanks for playing the Thirty Bells demo": the nights held, who is alive and who is lost, the dead put down, the trips and the buildings built, a line about the full game, and the buttons **Wishlist** (opens `wishlist_url`; not there while it is empty), **Play again** and **Quit to title**. A defeat is the ordinary game over. Saves work as before; in the demo Continue and Load keep to the demo's own saves.

**The data.** `assets/data/demo.json` (read through `scripts/demo.gd`): the seed and crew, the number of nights and the day of the Big Night, the buildings, the places and the road events that stay, the starting medicine, the size of nights 1 and 2 (`night_mult`) and of the Siege (`big_night`: its factor, brutes and cap), the day the briefing and the ideas begin, `wishlist_url` and the end card's words. Put the store page in `wishlist_url` to show the button. `tools/demo_lab.gd` plays the three nights with the harness's bot (`--modes=bot,idle --reps=0..5`) and says how many Big Nights were held with the player at the wall and without.

**The export.** `export_presets.cfg` has a second preset, **Web (demo)**, a copy of **Web** with the custom feature `demo` and the path `build/web-demo/index.html`:

```bash
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --headless --path . --export-release "Web (demo)" build/web-demo/index.html
```

Zip the contents of `build/web-demo` and upload it as its own itch.io page (or a second file of the page), as in Publishing below. The Web preset is unchanged.

## Saving

F5 saves to `user://quicksave.json` and F9 loads it. The game also saves to
`user://autosave.json` at each dawn (not in screenshot runs). A save is JSON and
holds the whole state of the rules, so a loaded game carries on the way the
saved one would have. A save that cannot be loaded (damaged, or from another
version) is refused and the game carries on. **Versions (SAVE4):** a One House save writes
`TownGen.VERSION` (`modules.scenario.town_version`); the town is made again from the seed, so a
save of an older town (no number, or a lower one: every save before this build) is
**incompatible**: Continue skips it, the Load page lists it greyed ("older version: cannot be
continued"), and loading it (a click, F9, `--load=path`) shows a card ("This save is from an older
version of Last Bell and cannot be continued. Start a new game.") and starts nothing. Any change
that alters the generated town or the saved state's meaning must bump `TownGen.VERSION`:
`docs/save-compat.md`.

## Balance harness

`tools/balance.gd` plays whole games without a window, with a sensible default
policy (repair the worn walls by day, send the best shooters scavenging when a
stock runs low, treat the infected), and prints one row per game and the average
colony day by day:

```bash
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/balance.gd -- --games=8 --days=10 --difficulty=1 --seed=1 [--fast] [--jobs=4] [--out=result.json]
```

`--fast` steps the rules at 15 Hz instead of 30, `--jobs=N` plays N games at a
time in worker processes, `--policy=none` gives no orders, `--policy=keep` hides the
crew in the school gym from the bell to dawn (the check that hiding does not beat
defending), `--policy=informed` plays the default policy and also reads the plan:
it stands the shooters where their guns cover the thinnest section and sets the alarm
(the check that knowing the night pays), `--sites=path` reads the scavenging sites from another file (to try a site before it is in the game), `--nostory` plays with the storyteller off (to see what the radio changes), `--verbose` lists what the policy did and who was lost; see the top of
the file for the rest. A game
whose colony collapses is slow to play (the walkers inside the yard re-path
every second).

With the avatar game (round 19) the harness plays it too: `--avatar=none` (the default: nobody is the player, the games of before the avatar), `--avatar=idle` (Ruth is the player and keeps her hands off in the school gym: the community alone holds the nights) and `--avatar=bot` (`tools/avatar_bot.gd` plays her: it walks to the wall the night will break on, shoots, answers the calls and mends by hand). Either avatar mode adds a row for each night (what the walls lost, who fell, how often the avatar stood where the pressure was) and a table of them to the summary; `--nightstats` gives the same without an avatar; `--data=path` reads another `nights.json`. Two more tools build on it: `tools/night_lab.gd` plays the same night with no avatar, an idle one and the bot from saved states, and `tools/community_probe.gd` plays avatar games and answers every idea the community brings (`--policy=accept|affordable|refuse`); their flags are at the top of each file. `python3 tools/compare_harness.py before.json after.json` says whether two harness outputs (`--out=`) are identical, ignoring only `took`.

## Tests

```bash
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/run.gd [-- --only=text]
```

Every `tests/test_*.gd` file is run; a test fails if it fails a check or logs an error. `tests/helpers.gd` builds a headless game for tests (`Helpers.make` plays with no avatar; `Helpers.avatar_game` sets one up; `Helpers.town_game(seed, {"home": true})` is a mini town with Ruth in the start house, where a real run begins at the town's entry). The suite is meant to be green: a test that fails because the game is wrong is left failing and listed in `tests/KNOWN_BUGS.md` (none on 2026-10-09). The runner clears the static callbacks a game leaves behind after each case.

**The no-avatar promise:** the simulation keeps a mode with nobody to play (`sim.player_id == ""`: the balance harness's default, `Helpers.make`, the classic Orientation Day the golden test plays). No player can start it (round 26: a real run, Orientation Day and every loaded save are avatar games; a save with no player gets Ruth, or the first of the living), and no flag asks for it; `Main.avatar_for_tests` is a tests-only switch for the helper scenes the tests build. That mode must play byte for byte as it did before the avatar game. `tests/test_nights_regression.gd` (against `tests/golden/harness_noavatar.json`) and `tests/test_round20_noavatar.gd` (whole-game digests, random state included) pin it; a failure there means a new rule is not behind avatar mode. Never re-pin to make one go away.
